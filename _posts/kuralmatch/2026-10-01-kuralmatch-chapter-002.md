## Comments and Research Notes

### Verse 1

Taittiriya Aranyaka (TA) 1.26.5.5,7 provides a particularly tight parallel to the Kural: आपो वै वृष्टिः, water is rain, and पर्जन्यो वर्षुको भवति, connecting that rain with Parjanya, the rain-bringing power of the heavens. This resonates with the kural's வானின் றுலகம் வழங்கி வருதலாற் றான்: since the rain that comes from the sky and sustains the world. TA 1.26.5.7 then states explicitly, अमृतं वा आपः, water indeed is amrita, directly corresponding to the kural's தானமிழ்தம். The following अमृतस्यानन्तरित्यै further emphasizes that water is the means of attaining or preserving the continuity of immortality, giving particular force to its designation as amrita. Finally, य एवं वेद, he who knows thus, closely resonates with என்றுணரற் பாற்று, the recognition that it is to be understood in this way. Thus both passages connect rain, the sustaining waters of skies, and amrita, presenting the rain that descends from the sky as the world's life-giving nectar.

### Verse 2

The Taittiriya Samhita verse 5.4.9.2 provides an excellent parallel for this Kural. It is immediately clear that अन्नमिव खलु वै वर्षम् (rain, indeed, is as it were food) is remarkably close to துப்பாயதூஉம் மழை (rain itself becomes food). The striking correspondence in rhythmic structure is hard to miss: अन्नस्यान्नस्य ... अन्नस्यान्नस्य ... अन्नमन्नम् ... वर्षम् repeatedly turns upon अन्न and then culminates in the single वर्षम्, just as the Kural repeatedly turns upon துப்பு and culminates in the single மழை.

The deeper semantic parallel becomes clearer once we extricate the relevant structure from the ritual machinery: अन्नस्यान्नस्यावरुद्ध्यै ... अवरुद्धं ह्यस्य अन्नम् \| अन्नमिव खलु वै वर्षम् \|. The later अवरुद्धम् connects back to the preceding अवरुद्ध्यै across the intervening ritual material. The ritual passage thus contains an underlying causal nexus: food is obtained for the oblation, food is offered as oblation (जुहोति), and the ritual nexus leads through rain to the securing of food and grain. Valluvar makes note of this ritual context a few verses later when he says that சிறப்பொடு பூசனை for the வானோர் can no longer proceed on earth if rain fails. The kural compresses a larger rain–ritual–food nexus into its economical two-part proposition: rain makes food for the food-eater, and rain itself becomes food.

The trichotomy of that-which-eats-food, that-which-becomes-food, and that-which-is-food in the kural's vocabulary of துப்பார், துப்பாயது, and துப்பு, has a striking analogue in the Vedic vocabulary of अन्नम्, अन्नाद्यम्, and अन्नादः. The Taittiriya tradition ultimately dissolves these distinctions into the unity of Brahman, allowing अन्नम् to encompass all three. This is expressed explicitly in Taittiriya Upanishad, Ananda-valli, 3.10.6, where the realized person declares: अहमन्नमहमन्नमहमन्नम् \| अहमन्नादोऽहमन्नादोऽहमन्नादः \|\| — “I am food ... I am the food-eater.” The passage therefore provides a particularly apt Sanskrit analogue for the Kural's ability to move between துப்பார், the eaters, and துப்பு, that which is eaten, while making the same semantic field encompass the thing that becomes food.

The अवरुद्ध्यै resonates with the ஆக்கி in துப்பாக்கி. This is corroborated by the Maitrayani Samhita, which explicitly connects वृष्टि, अन्नाद्य, and अवरुध्: in verse 2.4.8: वृष्टिर् वै देवेभ्योऽन्नाद्यम् अपाक्रामत् \| तेभ्यो वृष्टिम् अन्नाद्यम् अवारुन्द्ध \| ... वृष्ट्या अन्नाद्यस्यावरुद्ध्यै \|\| — “Rain, indeed, departed from the gods as that which becomes food; they secured rain as that which becomes food ... the rite is for securing that which becomes food through rain.” The verse provides independent Vedic corroboration of the துப்பாக்கி / துப்பாய movement in the Kural.

The trichotomy, and its tight association with rain, is also consistent with Kalingar's commentary on the kural: உண்பவர் யாவர்க்கும் உணவுப் பொருளாய் இன்னும் அவற்றை உளவாக உறுதிபண்ணிக் கொடுப்பதூஉஞ் செய்து; உண்பவர் யாவர்க்குந் தானே உணவுமாய் நிற்பதூஉம் மழை என்றவாறு. Kalingar first describes rain as providing and securing the food-substances for all who eat, and then explicitly says that rain itself stands as food for all who eat, matching my proposed reading of the kural and its parallel.

### Verse 5

Taittiriya Aranyaka (TA) 1.3.11 presents the same rain-dependent duality as the kural: या वै प्रजा भ्रश्यन्ते \| संवथ्सरात् ता भ्रश्यन्ते — those people who decline, decline from the year — corresponding to கெடுப்பதூஉம்; while याः प्रतिष्ठन्ति \| संवत्सरे ताः प्रतिष्ठन्ति — those who prosper/become established, are established in the year — corresponds to எடுப்பதூஉம். The repeated संवत्सर makes the agricultural, annual cycle explicit: when the rains fail, or are otherwise adverse, the people decline, while favorable rains sustain their establishment. And the concluding वर्षाभ्य इत्यर्थः, “the meaning is with reference to the rains, identifies rain as the force behind both outcomes, exactly as the kural assigns both கெடுப்பதும் and எடுப்பதும் to மழை. The TA leaves கெட்டார்க்குச் சார்வாய் implicit: the juxtaposition of भ्रश्यन्ते (decline/fall away) and प्रतिष्ठन्ति (become established) itself suggests the rain-dependent restoration of those who have fallen.

In Parimelazhakar’s commentary of the kural, பூமியின்கண் வாழ்வாரை (those who live upon the earth) fits naturally with the agricultural orientation of the TA’s प्रजा within this rain-governed annual cycle. He then makes the same duality explicit: பூமியின்கண் வாழ்வாரைப் பெய்யாது நின்று கெடுப்பதூஉம்; அவ்வாறு கெட்டார்க்குத் துணையாய்ப் பெய்து முன் கெடுத்தாற் போல எடுப்பதூஉம்; இவை எல்லாம் வல்லது மழை. 'எல்லாம்' என்றது, அம்மக்கள் முயற்சி வேறுபாடுகளால் கெடுத்தல் எடுத்தல்கள் தாம் பலவாதல் நோக்கி. 'வல்லது' என்பது அவாய் நிலையான் வந்தது. மழையினது ஆற்றல் கூறியவாறு. His concluding மழையினது ஆற்றல் கூறியவாறு (thus the power of rain has been stated) provides a striking corroboration of the TA’s वर्षाभ्य इत्यर्थः: in both readings, rain is the agency capable of producing either ruin or restoration.

### Verse 4

The kural  finds a close parallel in Amarushataka 57: कण्ठग्रहणम् corresponds to புல்லி, the act of embracing; स्थायिनि to விடா, staying without letting go; and मानस्य to புலவியுள்,  both denoting the offended reserve or feigned love-quarrel expressed. The inward response is given by मम चिन्ता, corresponding to என் உள்ளம், while भग्ना corresponds to உடைக்கும் and भवति to தோன்றும். The remaining image is especially suggestive: वज्रमय्याः depicts the nayika's resolve as vajra-like, while भग्ना is its breaking; correspondingly, படை can mean a weapon, implement, means, or agency, making the embrace itself the “weapon” or means that breaks her adamantine resolve. The dual sense of vajra—both the adamantine/diamond-like substance and the thunderbolt, a weapon—thus resonates particularly well with Valluvar's choice of படை.

The natural summary gloss given by the Kural Thiran website: காதலரைத் தழுவி விடாத ஊடலில் என்உள்ள உறுதியை உடைக்கும் கருவி தோன்றுகிறது corroborates this reading by unpacking the compressed kural as a situation in which, while the heroine remains in her ஊடல், there appears a means of breaking the firmness of her inner state: என் உள்ள உறுதியை உடைக்கும் கருவி. The gloss's உறுதி is especially close to the Sanskrit वज्रमय्याः, making explicit the quality that Valluvar leaves implicit in உள்ளம். 

The first half of the Amarushataka verse goes further by establishing the nayika's true mental and physical state: श्रुत्वा नामापि यस्य स्फुटघनपुलकं जायतेऽङ्गं समन्तात्दृष्ट्वा यस्याननेन्दुं भवति वपुरिदं चन्द्रकान्तानुकारि, that is, merely hearing his name causes goosebumps throughout her body, while seeing his face transforms her body into something resembling moonstone. Thus her मान or புலவி is a feigned love-quarrel rather than genuine resistance; given the intensity of her underlying attraction, it ultimately cannot withstand the nāyaka's कण्ठग्रहण or புல்லி, the embrace that becomes the “weapon” of its undoing.

### Verse 6

I propose Yoga Vasishtha 3.118.19 as the primary parallel for this kural. The expression सर्वाचारक्रमागतम् (conduct transmitted through the succession of established customs) corresponds to எவ்வ துறைவ துலகம், while आचारमाचरन्त्येव सुप्रबुद्धवदक्षतम् (they conduct themselves accordingly, just as one fully awakened would) corresponds to உலகத்தோடு அவ்வ துறைவ தறிவு. The reference to पार्श्वस्थबोधिताः (prompted by those around them) further reinforces the social dimension of the correspondence. सुप्रबुद्ध conveys the sense of being fully awake or awakened, illuminating the discernment implied by அறிவு. The correspondence is particularly close because the verse brings together both the established conduct of the world and the wisdom of conducting oneself in accordance with it.

The Taittiriya Upanishad 1.11.3–4 provides a corresponding injunction in Shruti, addressed to students completing their Vedic education: अथ यदि ते कर्मविचिकित्सा वा वृत्तविचिकित्सा वा स्यात् \| ये तत्र ब्राह्मणाः संमर्शिनः \| युक्ता आयुक्ताः \| अलूक्षा धर्मकामाः स्युः \| यथा ते तत्र वर्तेरन् \| तथा तत्र वर्तेथाः \|\|. The passage instructs the student that, should any doubt arise concerning an action or a matter of conduct, he should conduct himself as do the reflective, well-grounded, gentle, and dharma-oriented Brahmanas in that place. எவ்வ துறைவ துலகம் corresponds to यथा ते तत्र वर्तेरन्, the conduct of those in one's surroundings, while உலகத்தோடு அவ்வ துறைவ தறிவு corresponds to तथा तत्र वर्तेथाः, the discernment to shape one's own conduct accordingly. The Upanishad thus qualifies this emulation by directing the student to follow exemplary, dharma-oriented persons rather than conform indiscriminately to prevailing practice.

Manu Smriti 2.18 further corroborates the customary dimension of the principle: तस्मिन् देशे य आचारः पारम्पर्यक्रमागतः \| वर्णानां सान्तरालानां स सदाचार उच्यते \|\|. Manu identifies as सदाचार the conduct traditionally handed down in a particular country among its constituent social groups. The expression तस्मिन् देशे य आचारः corresponds to எவ்வ துறைவ துலகம், identifying the customary conduct prevailing in a particular country, while स सदाचार उच्यते establishes the discernment to conduct oneself in accordance with that accepted practice, as expressed in உலகத்தோடு அவ்வ துறைவ தறிவு.

A different, specifically relational and potentially political reading emerges from Vidura Niti, Mahabharata, Udyoga Parva 5.37.6 (BORI): यस्मिन्यथा वर्तते यो मनुष्यस्तस्मिंस्तथा वर्तितव्यं स धर्मः \| मायाचारो मायया वर्तितव्यः साध्वाचारः साधुना प्रत्युपेयः \|\|. One should behave towards a person in the manner in which that person behaves towards oneself: that is dharma. Deceit should be met with deceit, and good conduct should be reciprocated with good conduct. Here the correspondence is directly reciprocal: यस्मिन् यथा वर्तते यो मनुष्यः corresponds to எவ்வ துறைவ துலகம், while तस्मिंस्तथा वर्तितव्यम् corresponds to உலகத்தோடு அவ்வ துறைவ தறிவு. Unlike the preceding parallels, which concern conformity to established social or exemplary conduct, Vidura articulates a principle of reciprocity, extending even to the strategic response to deceit. This anticipates the modern tit-for-tat strategy in game theory, developed by Anatol Rapoport and popularized by Robert Axelrod's iterated Prisoner's Dilemma tournaments: one begins cooperatively and thereafter reciprocates the other party's previous action. Given the kural's placement in the அரசியல் portion of பொருட்பால், this reading is worth considering as a statecraft application—the discernment to deal with others in accordance with their own conduct. Vidura's formulation thus offers a more specifically relational and political interpretation of the broader principle expressed in the kural.

### Verse 9

I propose Kamandakiya Nitisara XII.20 and XII.24 as the primary parallel for this kural. Kamandaka identifies व्याधि (disease), alongside fire, water, famine, and pestilence, among the five natural calamities. He then assigns their prevention or remediation, together with the protection of the king and kingdom, to the minister. The lexical correspondence is particularly rich between எதிரதாக் காக்கும் and व्यसनस्य प्रतीकारो राजराज्याभिरक्षणम्, which means counteracting calamities and protecting the king and kingdom. Manakkudavar glosses எதிரதாக் காக்கும் as துன்பம் வருவதற்கு முன்பே வருமென்று நினைத்துக் காக்கும், while the dictionary defines எதிரதாக் காத்தல் as வரக்கடவதாகிய ஒன்றை முன்னே அறிந்து அதன் காரணத்தை விலக்குதல். The primary sense of எதிரதாக் is thus anticipation of what is yet to come, but எதிர் also carries the connotations of adversity and action directed against something. Correspondingly, व्यसन denotes calamity or adversity, while प्रतीकारः denotes counteraction, obviation, prevention, or remedy; the prefix प्रति further reinforces the sense of action directed against. The semantic range of காக்கும் is equally well accommodated: it encompasses protection (பாதுகாத்தல்), governance (அரசாளுதல்), anticipation (எதிர்பார்த்தல்), and removal or avoidance (விலக்குதல்). These converge in राजराज्याभिरक्षणम्, which signifies the comprehensive protection of king and kingdom. Thus the Sanskrit expression does not merely reproduce the idea of guarding against impending calamity; its constituent terms collectively accommodate the several interrelated senses embedded in Valluvar's compact expression.

Manakkudavar glosses அதிர வருவதோர் நோய் as நடுங்க வருவதொரு துன்பம். This corresponds to व्यसनम्, denoting calamity or adversity; the enumeration of the five प्रकृतिव्यसनानि in XII.20, with व्याधि providing the direct equivalent of நோய், makes the correspondence particularly precise. இல்லை finds its counterpart in हन्ति तद्व्यसनान्वितः, which states that the minister who undertakes this responsibility destroys the calamity, leaving no adversity to afflict the king or kingdom.

அறிவினார் is implicit in the ministerial responsibility expressed by अमात्यस्य कर्मेदम्. The qualification of the minister who can discharge this responsibility is illuminated by Kamandakiya IV.32: साधु तेषाम् अमात्यानां तद्विद्येभ्यश् च बुद्धिमान् \| चक्षुष्मत्तां च शिल्पं च परीक्षेत गुणद्वयम् \|\|. The prescription to examine an amatya for चक्षुष्मत्ता, understood in the technical Arthashastra sense as foresight (Apte, s.v. चक्षुष्मत्, sense 3, citing Kautilya, Arthashastra 1.9), helps explain the force of எதிரதாக் காக்கும் அறிவினார்: intelligence here is the capacity to perceive impending adversity and take measures against it in advance. The correspondence therefore brings out Valluvar's conception of intelligence not merely as the ability to respond to adversity, but as the foresight to prevent it.

Agni Purana 214.14–17, in its chapter on political science, reproduces verses almost identical to Kamandakiya Nitisara XII.20 and XII.24, with only minor textual and metrical variations. It thus provides an equivalent Puranic parallel to the same political principle.

At a more general philosophical level, Patanjali's Yogasutra 2.16 states हेयं दुःखमनागतम् — suffering that has not yet arisen is to be avoided. This expresses the underlying principle of preventing future suffering, but Valluvar gives it a specifically political and administrative application: the wise person anticipates adversity and guards against it through timely action. Kamandakiya Nitisara goes considerably further, elaborating in the surrounding chapters the various calamities that must be anticipated and the measures prescribed for their prevention or remediation. This detailed treatment illuminates the scope of Valluvar's otherwise cryptic reference to impending adversity.

    • Valluvar does not specify:
        ◦ What wanders: the mind, the senses, a person's conduct, a child, a friend, an administrative undertaking, or a constituent of the state.
        ◦ Where it wanders: towards sensory distractions, wrongful conduct, undesirable habits, a defective course of action, or a condition of decline.
        ◦ What causes the wandering: desire, ignorance, external influence, poor instruction, faulty judgment, defective administration, or adverse circumstances.
        ◦ How the wandering is restrained: self-discipline, discrimination, advice, instruction, correction, supervision, or remedial intervention.
        ◦ What constitutes the bad to be avoided: sin, harmful behaviour, unwholesome tendencies, an unprofitable course, or a state calamity.
        ◦ What constitutes the good to be sought: spiritual well-being, ethical conduct, the welfare of another, sound upbringing, or the prosperity of an undertaking or the state.
    • The subject, agency, cause, and objective are all left open. The operative principle remains constant: recognise an undesirable course, prevent its continuation, and direct it towards a beneficial one.
    • Its primary Sanskrit parallel in the Muktika Upanishad, consistent with the reading in the traditional Tamil commentaries, articulates this principle in the spiritual governance of mental tendencies. Bhartrihari applies a closely related corrective function to the true friend; the image of चित्तबालकम् in Muktika2.7 invites a pedagogical application to childrearing; and Kamandaka's treatment of प्रकृतिव्यसन provides an administrative application to the prevention and remediation of decline in the constituent elements of the state. The breadth of these applications is made possible by Valluvar's cryptic expression that does not specify what wanders, the cause or destination of its movement, the precise means of restraint, or what the good and bad outcomes signify. Although the traditional commentaries privilege the inward and spiritual interpretation, the kural's location within அரசியல் in பொருட்பால் makes its wider application to the conduct of public affairs especially significant.

        ◦ எப்பொருள் யார்யார்வாய்க் கேட்பினும் Whatever is heard, from whoever speaks. The source of the words must not determine  their credibility or value:
            ▪ Negative or potentially hostile sources (enemy, adversary, rival, critic, detractor): Do not dismiss what they say merely because of hostility; equally, do not trust it merely because it sounds agreeable.
            ▪ Positive or trusted sources (suru, friend, relative, minister, adviser, subordinate or staff): Respect the source, but do not surrender independent discernment. Even trusted persons may be mistaken, partial, or self-interested.
            ▪ Unknown or unverified sources (stranger, acquaintance, unfamiliar speaker, hearsay, anonymous or indirect report): Neither accept nor reject on reputation; establish the reliability and substance of what is communicated.

            ▪ Spiritual and philosophical discernment (guru's instruction, śruti, philosophical teaching, apparent versus deeper truth)	: Penetrate beyond the literal or superficial meaning to apprehend the underlying truth.
            ▪ Deciphering indirect communication (Cryptic warnings, symbolic speech, veiled advice, coded messages from well-wishers): Recognize the intended message beneath the expressed words; understand what circumstances may prevent the speaker from saying openly. 
            ▪ Psychological discernment (Hidden motives, anger, malice, fear, hurt, frustration, unmet needs): Understand the intention or emotional condition underlying someone's words or behaviour, without confusing inferred motive with established fact.
            ▪ Impartial extraction of beneficial truth (Insults, criticism, hostile speech, unpleasant advice, mixed statements containing truth and falsehood):	Set aside emotional reaction; separate the valid and beneficial substance from insult, distortion, or malice, retaining what is useful.
            ▪ Verification of reports and allegations (Gossip, accusations, reports concerning friends, relatives, associates, or rivals	): Investigate rather than react; distinguish fact from rumour, and truth from partiality or deliberate misrepresentation.

    • மெய்ப்பொருள் காண்பது அறிவு — the discernment exercised in five interpretations
        ◦ spiritual and philosophical discernment; 
        ◦ deciphering indirect communication; 
        ◦ psychological discernment; 
        ◦ impartial extraction of beneficial truth; and 
        ◦ verification of reports and allegations.
We shall organize the corroborating texts around the five dimensions of மெய்ப்பொருள் காணுதல், since each dimension necessarily involves consideration of both யார்யார்வாய் and எப்பொருள். The speaker's identity and relationship, the nature and form of the speech, and its subject matter all affect the circumstances in which discernment is exercised. 
1. Spiritual and philosophical discernment. Examine how one penetrates beyond the apparent or literal meaning of a teaching to establish its deeper truth. The speaker may be a guru or teacher, the śruti, or another philosophical authority; nevertheless, the teaching must be examined through inquiry. Use Yogavāsiṣṭha 2.18.6 and Vivekacūḍāmaṇi 13 as corroborations of the primary parallel.
2. Deciphering indirect communication. Examine cryptic warnings, symbolic speech, veiled advice, and coded messages from well-wishers. Here the speaker's relationship and intention are especially important, as are the form and apparent meaning of the utterance. Use Vidura's coded counsel to Yudhiṣṭhira in Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, BORI Critical Edition, Adhyāya 133, as the narrative illustration.
3. Psychological discernment. Examine the ability to infer hidden motives and emotional states—anger, malice, fear, hurt, frustration, or unmet needs—from speech and outward behaviour. This applies across positive, negative, and neutral relationships, and requires distinguishing what is actually expressed from what may be inferred. Use Pañcatantra 1.44–45.
4. Impartial extraction of beneficial truth. Examine the ability to separate useful or true substance from insults, criticism, hostile speech, unpleasant advice, or statements mixing truth and falsehood. The speaker may be an adversary, a friend, or a trusted adviser; the manner of delivery must not obscure the substance. Use Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa 6.36.1 as a negative illustration of Rāvaṇa's failure to recognize Mālyavān's beneficial counsel. Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva 4.51.15 and Manu Smṛti 2.239 can corroborate the broader principle of recognizing merit even in an enemy.
5. Verification of reports and allegations. Examine gossip, accusations, and reports concerning friends, relatives, associates, or rivals. The source may be trusted, hostile, or unknown; the report may be pleasant, unpleasant, or apparently neutral. Wisdom requires investigation rather than acceptance based on the reporter's identity or the emotional effect of the allegation. Use Arthaśāstra 3.1.46 as the procedural corroboration.  Present Arthaśāstra 1.2.11 as the broader intellectual framework of आन्वीक्षिकी—reasoned examination of right and wrong, gain and loss, policy and its contrary, strength and weakness.

### Prasada in literature and performing arts on clarity of expression

### Robustness principle in modern communications technology
    • While not directly the intended reading of the kural, a computer networking maxim is worth mentioning.     • Brihat Canakya Nīti 10.1 
        ◦ धनहीनो न हीनश्च धनिकः स सुनिश्चयः । विद्यारत्नेन हीनो यः स हीनः सर्ववस्तुषु ॥
        ◦ The person without wealth is not truly deficient; he should be regarded as wealthy. But the person who lacks the jewel of learning is deficient in every respect.
        ◦ The decisive correspondence is the second line: विद्यारत्नेन हीनो यः स हीनः सर्ववस्तुषु — one who lacks the jewel of vidyā is deficient in every respect. This closely expresses the negative half of the Kural: அறிவிலா ரென்னுடைய ரேனு மிலர். The positive half, அறிவுடையா ரெல்லா முடையார், is implicit in the contrast. 
        ◦ We should also state that vidyā here need not be restricted to education or technical skill; it can be read as discriminative knowledge and wisdom. That is a contextual interpretation, not a claim that the Sanskrit word always has that restricted meaning.
        ◦ Kalingar’s gloss bridges the apparent gap between vidyā read as education and அறிவு:
            ▪ காலிங்கர்: கற்றும் கேட்டும் கசடு அற்ற அறிவுடையோரை மற்று எல்லாம் உடையரென்று உணரப்படும்;ஆதலால் மற்று அங்ஙனம் தெளிந்த அறிவிலாதார் வேறு யாதானும் உடையரேனும் யாதும் இலர் என்றவாறு. 
            ▪ Learning and listening, when they produce கசடற்ற அறிவு, yield the wisdom that constitutes true possession

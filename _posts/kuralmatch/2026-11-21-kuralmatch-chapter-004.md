    • Take Manusmṛti 4.14 as the primary parallel: वेदोदितं स्वकं कर्म नित्यं कुर्यादतन्द्रितः ... यथाशक्ति. First explain the semantic range of the Kural, especially the several possible senses of வகை, and how these allow more than one reading of வகையான் அறவினை. Develop two readings alongside वेदोदितं स्वकं कर्म: (1) the fivefold classification of dharma in Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 181.10–15, particularly its connection with वेदधर्मः, with Agni Purāṇa 166 mentioned briefly as a related attestation; and (2) the alternative classification in Chāndogya Upaniṣad 2.23.1, त्रयो धर्मस्कन्धाः. It is not practical to corroborate the kural against all possible readings of vakai,  we give selected examples: Manu 4.21 for yajna and a closer Sanskrit parallel, if one can be found, for dāna. BG 17.20 remains a fallback rather than a settled choice.
    • Manusmriti 4.14

        ◦ Correspondence:
            ▪ वेदोदितं स्वकं कर्म corresponds to ஒல்லும் வகையா னறவினை, the karma suitable to one’s kind as per the Vedas

    • The kural admits several layered interpretaions due to versatile word choices and we should do a matrix and find matches for multiple interpretations.
        ◦ ஒல்லு can mean இயலு (be possible, practicable) as well as இணங்கு (agree, suit) 
        ◦ செல்லும் can mean இயலும் (what’s one is capable of), செய்யத்தகும் (what should be done), எய்தும் (where one goes)
        ◦ அறவினை can mean dharma-karya, svadharma, svakarma, yajna, or dana
        ◦ வாய் means இடம் (a place, a situation, or merely signify the locative case); வாக்கு/சொல் (speech/words); the opening of something that suggests reception, i.e., where something goes), விதம் (mode, manner), and மெய் (truth) 
        ◦ வகை can apply to the அறவினை, but also to the person doing it, the target or context of the action.
        ◦ We will focus on ஒல்லும் வகையா னறவினை as read in the sense of वेदोदितं स्वकं कर्म, and provide two ramifications of this reading

    • Chandogya Upanishad 2.23.1
        ◦ त्रयो धर्मस्कन्धा यज्ञोऽध्ययनं दानमिति प्रथमस्तप एव द्वितीयो ब्रह्मचार्याचार्यकुलवासी तृतीयोऽत्यन्तमात्मानमाचार्यकुलेऽवसादयन्सर्व एते पुण्यलोका भवन्ति ब्रह्मसंस्थोऽमृतत्वमेति ॥ There are three divisions of dharma: The first comprises sacrifices, study, and charity; the second consists of austerities; and the third is the life of celibacy and living with the teacher in his house till death. People devoted to these three divisions of religion go to heaven after death. But one who is devoted to Brahman attains immortality.
    • Bhavishya Purana Brahma-parva 181.10 allows us to read dharma as being five kinds
        ◦ वर्णधर्मः स्मृतस्त्वेक आश्रमाणामतः परम् । वर्णाश्रमस्तृतीयस्तु गौणो नैमित्तिकस्तथा ॥
        ◦ So we can see वेदोदितं स्वकं कर्म as including:
            ▪ 1. वर्णधर्म — duties according to varṇa;
            ▪ 2. आश्रमधर्म — duties according to ashrama;
            ▪ 3. वर्णाश्रमधर्म — duties according to both;
            ▪ 4. नैमित्तिकधर्म — occasional or occasion-specific duties;
            ▪ 5. गुणधर्म — duties according to qualifications.
        ◦ A similar attestation, with minor differences, is also seen in Agni Purana 166.1-5

| [4](#verse-4) | மனத்துக்கண் மாசில னாத லனைத்தற <br> னாகுல நீர பிற |  |

    • Mahabharata, Vana Parva 3.203.96–97
        ◦ त्रिदण्डधारणं मौनं जटाभारोऽथ मुण्डनम् । वल्कलाजिनसंवेष्टं व्रतचर्याऽभिषेचनम् ॥ अग्निहोत्रं वने वासः शरीरपरिशोषणम् । सर्वाण्येतानि मिथ्या स्युर्यदि भावो न निर्मलः ॥
        ◦ Carrying the triple staff, observing silence, wearing matted hair or shaving the head, wearing bark and deerskin, practising vows and ritual bathing; performing Agnihotra, dwelling in the forest and mortifying the body—all these are false if one's inner disposition is not pure.
        ◦ Manakkudavar: ஒருவன் தன்மனத்தின்கட் குற்றமிலனாதலே எல்லாவறமுமாம்; அதில் அழுக்குண்டாயின் மேற்செய்வன வெல்லாம் ஆரவார நீர்மைய. பிறரறியவேண்டிச் செய்தானாமென்றவாறாயிற்று.
    • Vamana Purana, Saro-mahatmya 43.75, 79–81, 86 (chapter numbering varies by edition)
        ◦ अहो मुग्धा यदा यूयं क्रोधेन कलुषीकृताः । न धर्मस्य क्रिया काचिज्ज्ञायते मूढबुद्धयः ।श्रूयतां धर्मसर्वस्वं तापसाः क्रूरचेष्टिताः ॥ ७५ ॥ ततः कर्मवशाद् भुङ्क्ते सम्भोगान् स्वर्गनारकान् । तन्मनः शोधयेद् धीमान् ज्ञानयोगाद्युपक्रमैः ॥ ७९ ॥ तस्मिन् शुद्धे ह्यन्तरात्मा स्वयमेव निराकुलः । न शरीरस्य सङ्क्लेशैरपि निर्दहनात्मकैः ॥ ८० ॥ शुद्धिमाप्नोति पुरुषः संशुद्धं यस्य नो मनः । क्रिया हि नियमार्थाय पातकेभ्यः प्रकीर्तिताः ॥ ८१ ॥ यत्क्रोधनो यजति यच्च ददाति नित्यं यद्वा तपस्तपति यच्च जुहोति तस्य । प्राप्नोति नैव किमपीह फलं हि लोके मोघं फलं भवति तस्य हि क्रोधनस्य ॥ ८६ ॥
        ◦ The Vamana Purana’s narrative comes remarkably close to Valluvar’s reasoning, and also offers striking lexical correspondences.
        ◦ The context: The sages, though engaged in religious observances, are overcome by anger. Brahma rebukes them for failing to understand धर्मसर्वस्वम्, the whole of dharma. He directs them to purify the mind, तन्मनः शोधयेत्, and declares that, with the mind purified, the inner self is निराकुलः, free from agitation. The Purana further explains that Dharmic observances क्रिया are prescribed as disciplines to restrain sinful conduct, and when performed by an anger-afflicted person yield no fruit.
        ◦ The correspondence to the kural:
            ▪ तन्मनः शोधयेत्, reinforced by तस्मिन् शुद्धे and संशुद्धं, expresses the purification of mind, மனத்துக்கண் மாசிலன் ஆதல்.
            ▪ धर्मसर्वस्वम्, the whole of dharma, echoes அனைத்தறன், while क्रिया हि नियमार्थाय पातकेभ्यः, the Dharmic observances prescribed as restraints against sin, gives substance to Valluvar’s compact பிற. Their fruitlessness when performed in anger indicates the absence of संशुद्धं and, consequently, of the निराकुलः state. Therefore, without mental purity, all of these Dharmic observances, धर्मसर्वस्वम् … क्रिया that correspond to அனைத்தறன் … பிற are associated with आकुलत्व, that is, ஆகுல நீர.  
Interestingly, निराकुलः (निर् + आकुल) and ஆகுல நீர exhibit a striking phonetic correspondence, with their constituent sounds appearing in reverse order; this may, of course, be coincidental. Note: the words in the kural are glossed as follows: ஆகுல - ஆரவாரம், ஆடம்பரம், இரைச்சல், வெளிப்பகட்டு; நீர-தன்மையுடையன; பிற-மற்றவை.

| [7](#verse-7) | அறத்தா றிதுவென வேண்டா சிவிகை <br> பொறுத்தானோ டூர்ந்தா னிடை |  |

Vishnu Purana 2.13.82, 92 
सर्वस्यैव हि भूपाल जन्तोः सर्वत्र कारणम् । धर्माधर्मौ यतः कस्मात्कारणं पृच्छ्यते त्वया ॥८२॥
त्वं राजा शिबिका चेयमिमे वाहाः पुराःसुराः । अयञ्च भवतो लोको न सदेतन्नृपोच्यते ॥९२॥

The kural invites two complementary readings of அறத்தா றிதுவென வேண்டா: there is no need to say that the difference between the palanquin-bearer and the rider is the result of dharma, either because the cause is evident or because that attribution itself may be rejected. In the first reading, வேண்டா conveys that no explanation need be supplied: the contrast between the two men is itself sufficient evidence of the operation of dharma and adharma. In the second, it is a denial: one should not declare the difference between their stations to be the fruit of dharma. 

The distinction between these readings is reflected in two commentarial interpretations. Kalingar, in the traditional commentary, explains உலகத்து அறத்தின் நெறி இத்தன்மைத்து என்று உமக்கு யாம் எடுத்துரைக்க வேண்டுவதில்லை; சிவிகையைச் சுமந்தவனோடு மற்று அதன்மேலேறியிருக்கின்றவனுமே சான்று—there is no need to explain the way of dharma, since the bearer and the one seated upon the palanquin themselves bear witness to it. Kunrakkudi Adigalar, in a modern interpretation, by contrast, understands இதுவென வேண்டா** as a rejection of the customary social explanation that the difference between the one who carries the palanquin and the one who rides in it is the fruit of dharma. He reads the expression as a வியங்கோள் of negation, refusing to accept the suffering imposed upon one person for the benefit of another as a legitimate consequence of dharma: சிவிகை ஊர்ந்து செல்வோன் - தூக்கிச் செல்வோனிடையில் உள்ள வேறுபாட்டை அறத்தின் பயன் என்று கூறும் பழக்கம் உடைய சமுதாயத்தை மறுத்துக் கூறியது. 

The Jada Bharata episode in the Vishnu Purana provides an illuminating narrative and philosophical counterpart to both readings. King Rahugana travels in a palanquin carried by bearers, among whom is Jada Bharata, an enlightened sage. The narrative first presents the contrast in the same concrete terms as the kural: the king is seated in the palanquin, शिविकारूढः (2.13.53), while Jada Bharata joins the other bearers and carries the king's palanquin (उवाह शिविकाम्, 2.13.55). He does so with the desire to exhaust the effects of his past sins (पापस्य क्षयकामः, 2.13.56). When the king notices the uneven movement of the palanquin, he orders the bearers to proceed evenly (2.13.58). The encounter leads to Bharata's instruction on the nature of embodied existence and the apparent distinctions between persons.

The first primary parallel, Vishnu Purana 2.13.82, directly resonates with Kalingar's interpretation: सर्वस्यैव हि भूपाल जन्तोः सर्वत्र कारणम् । धर्माधर्मौ यतः कस्मात्कारणं पृच्छ्यते त्वया ॥८२॥ For every embodied being, O king, dharma and adharma are the causes of the circumstances experienced; why, then, do you ask for a particular cause? The expression सर्वत्र कारणम् and the rhetorical question कस्मात्कारणं पृच्छ्यते त्वया are especially significant alongside அறத்தா றிதுவென வேண்டா. The Purana explains that the differing experiences of embodied beings arise from dharma and adharma; there is therefore no need to ask for a separate explanation of their circumstances. This corresponds to the kural's first reading: the contrast between the bearer and the rider is intelligible within the ordinary operation of karmic causation. The narrative supplies the concrete illustration, while verse 82 articulates its underlying principle. This is the vyavaharika perspective: the differences are experienced as real within worldly existence, and their causes are understood through dharma and adharma.

The second primary parallel, Vishnu Purana 2.13.92, takes the argument beyond this conventional account:  त्वं राजा शिबिका चेयमिमे वाहाः पुराःसुराः । अयञ्च भवतो लोको न सदेतन्नृपोच्यते ॥९२॥Shridhara explains the philosophical purpose of this verse: ननु शिबिकाद्यचेतनभेदस्य वाह्यवाहकादिचेतनभेदस्य च दृश्यमानत्वात् कथमात्मनः सजातीय-विजातीयभेदो नास्तीत्याशङ्क्य तद्भेदस्य मिथ्यात्वम् आह—त्वं राज इत्यादिना । The apparent distinctions between insentient things such as the palanquin and sentient beings such as the carried and the bearers are indeed visible. The king might therefore question how distinctions between beings of the same or different kinds can be denied. Shridhara identifies the visible distinction between the one who is carried and those who carry him as वाह्यवाहकादिचेतनभेद, directly corresponding to the kural's பொறுத்தானோ டூர்ந்தா னிடை. Although this distinction is empirically apparent, Bharata's response establishes तद्भेदस्य मिथ्यात्वम्—its ultimate unreality. This provides a deeper philosophical dimension to Kunrakkudi Adigalar's rejection of the customary attribution of the difference between bearer and rider to the fruit of dharma. While Adigalar questions the social distinction itself, the Purāṇa extends the argument to the unreality of distinctions among beings, सजातीय-विजातीयभेद, at the level of the Self.

The Purana reconciles the two interpretations through an Advaitic distinction between the vyavaharika and paramarthika levels: the apparent differences in worldly circumstances arise from dharma and adharma, yet those very distinctions are ultimately mithya and unreal in the Self. This resolves the apparent contradiction between Kalingar's karmic explanation and Kunrakkudi Adigalar's refutation of the social attribution. The narrative reinforces this insight through Jada Bharata, the enlightened sage who appears as the bearer, while the king seated in the palanquin is himself instructed by him.

The Buddhist text, Karma-vibhanga (70b) poses the question, कतमे कर्मणो विपाकेन राजा चक्रवर्ती हस्त्यश्वादीनि वाहनानि प्रतिलभते?—by the fruition of what action does one become a universal monarch and obtain elephants, horses and other conveyances? The question's कर्मणो विपाकेन resonates with the Kural's அறத்தா றிதுவென. The answer includes carrying one's parents or teacher on one's shoulders or conveying them in palanquins (स्कन्धेन वहति … शिबिकायानैर्वा वहति) as the meritorious action whose fruit is royal status and conveyances. Thus, one who once bore others through meritorious service may, in a later existence, enjoy the privilege of being borne.

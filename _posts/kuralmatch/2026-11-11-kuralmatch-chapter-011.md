## Comments and Research Notes

### Verse 1

I propose Mahabharata, Shanti Parva 12.138.82 (Gita Press) as the primary parallel for this kural. The expression पूर्वोपकारिणा corresponds directly to செய்யாமற் செய்த உதவிக்கு, identifying the original benefactor whose act of kindness was not itself a repayment for any prior favor. The juxtaposition with प्रत्युपकुर्वन्, one who renders a favor in return, accentuates this distinction between original benefaction and reciprocation. Further, बह्वपि न भाति explicitly states that even abundant reciprocation cannot equal the original benefaction; Valluvar conveys the same immeasurability metaphorically through வையகமும் வானகமு மாற்ற லரிது, invoking the earth and heavens as measures that cannot match it. The concluding निष्कारणमेव कुरुते further anticipates பயன்றூக்கார் செய்த உதவி in a subsequent kural, emphasizing that the original benefaction is rendered without calculating or expecting a return.

A thematic corroboration occurs in the Mahasubhāṣitasaṅgraha (verse 7015), which distinguishes three dispositions toward beneficence: उपकारपरः प्रवरः प्रत्युपकारं करोति मध्यमः \|
नीचस्तदपि न कुरुते उपकारवशाद्भवति शत्रुः सः \|\|. One who is naturally devoted to helping others is superior; one who renders help in reciprocation is middling; the lowly person does not even return a favor and instead becomes an enemy on account of the obligation incurred. The distinction places the unsolicited benefaction of செய்யாமற் செய்த உதவி at the highest level, while treating प्रत्युपकार as a lesser, though proper, response. This verse thus corroborates the hierarchy implicit in the Mahabharata parallel, although its emphasis is on the relative dispositions of benefactors and recipients rather than on the immeasurability of the original favor.

Bhagavata Purana 8.23.2 offers an interesting, though oblique, connection through Bali's acknowledgment of the extraordinary grace bestowed upon him by Vamana: अहो प्रणामाय कृतः समुद्यमः प्रपन्नभक्तार्थविधौ समाहितः \| यल्लोकपालैस्त्वदनुग्रहोऽमरैरलब्धपूर्वोऽपसदेऽसुरेऽर्पितः \|\|. Bali acknowledges that he had merely attempted to offer obeisance (प्रणामाय कृतः समुद्यमः), yet received a favor (त्वदनुग्रहः) previously unattained even by the guardians of the worlds and the gods (लोकपालैः ... अमरैरलब्धपूर्वः), bestowed upon an insignificant asura such as himself (अपसदेऽसुरेऽर्पितः). This extraordinary grace, received without any commensurate prior benefaction on Bali's part, resonates with செய்யாமற் செய்த உதவி. Bhagavata Purana 8.20.33–34 describes the Lord's cosmic strides: मधुव्रतस्रग्वनमालयावृतो रराज राजन्भगवानुरुक्रमः \| क्षितिं पदैकेन बलेर्विचक्रमे नभः शरीरेण दिशश्च बाहुभिः \|\| पदं द्वितीयं क्रमतस्त्रिविष्टपं न वै तृतीयाय तदीयमण्वपि \| उरुक्रमस्याङ्घ्रिरुपर्युपर्यथो महर्जनाभ्यां तपसः परं गतः \|\|. The Lord strides across the earth with one step (क्षितिम्), encompasses the sky with His body (नभः), and the directions with His arms; with the second step He encompasses heaven (त्रिविष्टपम्), leaving no space for the third. Having received from Vishnu the extraordinary grace described as अनुग्रहोऽमरैरलब्धपूर्वः, Bali offers Vamana three paces of land, which the Lord then covers in His cosmic strides. Yet the earth (क्षितिम्) and heaven (त्रिविष्टपम्), corresponding to வையகமும் வானகமும், prove inadequate to accommodate the third stride (न वै तृतीयाय तदीयमण्वपि), whereupon Bali offers his own head. This is not the direct import of the kural, but the imagery strikingly echoes வையகமும் வானகமும் ஆற்றலரிது: even these vast domains prove insufficient.


### Verse 2

I propose Yoga Vasishtha 1.7.26 as the primary parallel for this kural. The correspondence is remarkably close: காலத்தினால் செய்த நன்றி finds its direct counterpart in काले कृतम् and उपकार; சிறிதெனினும் corresponds to अण्वपि, while the resulting greatness of the benefit is expressed through उपकारताम्. The Sanskrit verse makes the converse explicit as well: महदप्युपकारोऽपि रिक्ततामेत्यकालतः — even substantial assistance becomes ineffective when rendered at the wrong time. Valluvar emphasizes the immense worth of even a small benefit conferred at the appropriate time, leaving this converse implicit. The Yoga Vasishtha thus articulates both sides of the principle, although it does not employ Valluvar's striking comparison with the magnitude of the world, ஞாலத்தின் மாணப் பெரிது.

A complementary ethical perspective appears in Bhartrihari's Niti Shatakam 73, which enumerates the characteristics of a true friend: पापान्निवारयति योजयते हिताय गुह्यं निगूहति गुणान् प्रकटिकरोति \| आपद्गतं च न जहाति ददाति काले सन्मित्रलक्षणमिदं प्रवदन्ति सन्तः \|\|. Among the qualities enumerated for a good friend, ददाति काले (gives at the appropriate time) directly resonates with காலத்தினாற்செய்த நன்றி of this kural. Bhartrihari's verse is particularly valuable when read alongside the other kurals in this chapter, for its enumeration of the qualities of a true friend, सन्मित्रलक्षणमिदं, resonates with several of Valluvar's formulations. आपद्गतं च न जहाति (does not abandon one in adversity) recalls kural 6, துறவற்க துன்பத்துட் டுப்பாயார் நட்பு, while पापान्निवारयति (prevents one from doing wrong) echoes kural 7, விழுமந் துடைத்தவர் நட்பு.  Bhartrihari thus brings together, within his description of a true friend, three dimensions of friendship that Valluvar develops across these successive kurals: timely assistance, steadfastness in adversity, and the removal of distress.


### Verse 3

I propose Mahabharata, Shanti Parva 12.138.82 as the primary parallel for this kural too (along with kural 1). பயன்றூக்கார் செய்த உதவி finds its counterpart in निष्कारणमेव कुरुते: the original benefactor renders assistance without calculating or weighing any benefit to be gained in return. Valluvar then asks us to weigh the benefaction (நயன்றூக்கி) and declares its benefit greater than the ocean (நன்மை கடலிற் பெரிது). The Mahabharata expresses this act of comparison through प्रत्युपकुर्वन्बह्वपि न भाति पूर्वोपकारिणा तुल्यः: even abundant reciprocation does not measure up to the original benefactor. The expressions न भाति and तुल्यः resonate with Valluvar's தூக்கின் and நயன்றூக்கி, while the comparison itself differs: Valluvar measures the magnitude of the benefaction using the vastness of the ocean as a metaphor, whereas the Mahabharata measures it against even the most generous reciprocation. The verse occurs in the Marjara–Mushika dialogue, in which the mouse rescues the cat and the latter promises to repay him; Bhishma draws from their exchange this general principle about the incomparable worth of an unmotivated benefaction.

The following verses further illuminate பயன்றூக்கார் செய்த உதவி through the ideal of परोपकार rendered निरपेक्ष—assistance to others without expectation of return. They characterize such benefactors as उत्तमाः, सत्सु, विरलाः and उन्नतचेतसाम् (excellent, virtuous, rare, and noble-minded):
  - Vallabhadeva’s Subhashitavali, verse 224: अपेक्षन्ते न च स्नेहं न पात्रं न दशान्तरम् \| सदा लोकहिते युक्ता रत्नदीपा इवोत्तमाः \|\|. Excellent people, like luminous jewel-lamps, remain devoted to the welfare of the world; they do not look for affection, the recipient’s worthiness, or favorable circumstances. The expressions न च स्नेहं न पात्रं न दशान्तरम् illuminate பயன்றூக்கார்: the excellent do not weigh affection, the recipient’s worthiness, or the circumstances in rendering assistance. सदा लोकहिते युक्ताः resonates with செய்த உதவி, expressing their abiding commitment to the welfare of the world. Finally, रत्नदीपा इवोत्तमाः, great as a jewel-lamp, offers an image of their exceptional worth, complementing Valluvar’s comparison of the benefaction as larger than the ocean: நயன்றூக்கி நன்மை கடலிற் பெரிது. 
  - Vallabhadeva’s Subhashitavali, verse 226: नालोकः क्रियते सूर्ये भूः प्रतीपं न धार्यते \| नहि प्रत्युपकाराणामपेक्षा सत्सु विद्यते \|\|. The sun does not withhold its light, nor does the earth turn away; indeed, the good have no expectation of reciprocation. The closing statement, नहि प्रत्युपकाराणामपेक्षा सत्सु विद्यते, resonates with the ideal of பயன்றூக்கார் செய்த உதவி, rendered without weighing its return.
  - Sarasvatikantahbharana verse XX: विरला उपकृत्यैव निरपेक्षा जलधरा इव वर्तन्ते \| क्षीयन्ते तेषां विरहे विरला एव सरित्प्रवाहा इव \|\|. Rare indeed are those who, having rendered help, remain free of expectation, like the clouds. In their absence, the streams dwindle and become scarce. The rare benefactors who render assitance without expectation (विरला उपकृत्यैव निरपेक्षा) illuminates பயன்றூக்கார், depicting those who render உதவி without weighing what they might receive in return. The subsequent image of dwindling streams in their absence conveys how greatly others depend upon such benefactors.
  - Subhashitaratnabhandagara, Samanya-prakarana, Sajjana-prashansa 184: न चन्द्रमाः प्रत्युपकारलिप्सया करोति भाभिः कुमुदावबोधनम् \| स्वभाव एवोन्नतचेतसामयं परोपकारव्यसनं हि जीवितम् \|\|. The moon does not awaken the water-lilies with its rays out of a desire for reciprocation. Beneficence towards others is simply the nature of the noble-minded; indeed, it is their very way of life. The benefaction without desire for reciprocation (प्रत्युपकारलिप्सया करोति) resonates with பயன்றூக்கார் ெய்த உதவி, and such peope are further characterized by their nature to help others स्वभावः परोपकारव्यसनम्.
 

### Verse 4

I propose Chanakya Niti Sutra 6.29 as the primary parallel for this kural. The correspondence is remarkably close in both thought and imagery: தினைத்துணை finds its counterpart in तिलमात्रम्  (a sesame seed, just as தினை denotes a small millet seed), while பனைத்துணையாகக் கொள்வர் is expressed as शैलमात्रं मन्यते  (regarding it as a mountain, just as பனை denotes a palm tree); similarly, பயன்றெரி வார் corresponds to साधुः, the virtuous person who recognizes the worth of a benefaction. Both verses extol the disposition to magnify even the smallest assistance received. The minor variation in imagery serves the respective poetic expressions and rhyme; it does not affect the close correspondence in thought.

The Mahabharata offers a complementary corroboration in Anushasana Parva 13.182.12 (Gita Press): अल्पोऽपि तादृशो न्यासो भवत्युत महाफलः \| तृषिताय च यद्दत्तं हृदयेनानसूयता \|\|. The correspondence with the kural can be drawn more finely through the description of the recipient and the disposition involved: திணைத்துணை நன்றி செயினும் corresponds to अल्पोऽपि तादृशो न्यासः, while பயன்றெரி வார் finds a suggestive counterpart in तृषिताय ... हृदयेनानसूयता — one who recognizes the worth of a small benefit is here understood through the example of a thirsty person receiving a gift offered with a heart free from envy or ill will. The verse occurs in the dialogue between Vyasa and Maitreya, narrated by Bhishma in response to Yudhishthira's question as to which is superior among learning, austerity, and giving (13.182.1–2). Vyasa, traveling incognito in Varanasi, is received by Maitreya, who honors him and offers him food (13.182.3–5). Pleased by the reception, Vyasa smiles, prompting Maitreya to ask the reason. Vyasa explains that he is reflecting on the extraordinary merit of a seemingly modest act of giving. The subsequent discussion establishes the greatness of giving and the importance of the disposition with which it is performed. Both the Mahbharata verse and the Kural convey that even an apparently small benefaction may be of great value, as discerned by the recipient.

### Verse 10

I propose Valmiki Ramayanam, Kishkindha Kandam 4.33.12 as the primary parallel for this kural. The correspondence is remarkably close: செய்ந்நன்றி கொன்ற மகற்கு finds its direct equivalent in कृतघ्ने, while உய்வில்லை is expressed almost verbatim as नास्ति निष्कृतिः; correspondingly, உய்வுண்டாம் is answered by निष्कृतिर्विहिता सद्भिः. Valluvar generalizes the question to those who destroy all forms of goodness, எல்லா நன்மைகளையுஞ் சிதைத்தார்க்கும் in Mankkudavar's gloss, whereas the Ramayana enumerates particular grave transgressions for which expiation is prescribed — ब्रह्मघ्ने च सुरापे च चोरे भग्नव्रते तथा — before singling out ingratitude as beyond expiation. Neither text, of course, condones the other transgressions; the contrast serves to emphasize the exceptional gravity of कृतघ्नता. A slight textual variation occurs in the Southern Recension at 4.34.12, which reads गो घ्ने च एव in place of ब्रह्मघ्ने च. The central formulation, कृतघ्ने नास्ति निष्कृतिः, remains unchanged.

The surrounding verses provide valuable contextual corroboration.  The sequence begins with the general condemnation of one who fails to repay the assistance of friends: पूर्वं कृतार्थो मित्राणां न तत् प्रतिकरोति यः \| कृतघ्नः सर्वभूतानां स वध्यः प्लवगेश्वर \|\| (4.33.10). This is followed by the introduction of the celebrated verse attributed to Brahma: गीतोऽयं ब्रह्मणा श्लोकः सर्वलोकनमस्कृतः \| दृष्ट्वा कृतघ्नं क्रुद्धेन तं निबोध प्लवंगम \|\| (4.33.11), leading directly to the primary parallel quoted above. The admonition then becomes personal and explicit: अनार्यस्त्वं कृतघ्नश्च मिथ्यावादी च वानर \| पूर्वं कृतार्थो रामस्य न तत्प्रतिकरोषि यत् \|\| (4.33.13). Sugriva is being admonished for failing to reciprocate Rama's assistance after having obtained his own desired end.

The Mahabharata independently corroborates the same formulation in Shanti Parva 12.167.19 (BORI), with a parallel occurrence in Udyoga Parva 5.105.10 (BORI): कुतः कृतघ्नस्य यशः कुतः स्थानं कुतः सुखम् \| अश्रद्धेयः कृतघ्नो हि कृतघ्ने नास्ति निष्कृतिः \|\|. The verse asks how an ungrateful person can have fame, standing, or happiness, declaring such a person untrustworthy and reiterating that there is no expiation for ingratitude. The concluding phrase कृतघ्ने नास्ति निष्कृतिः directly corroborates Valluvar's உய்வில்லை செய்ந்நன்றி கொன்ற மகற்கு.

    • I am thinking Chanakya Niti should be the primary parallel. In fact we can explain the nature of both matter in this kural. Then we present the Nala episode saying the size of the reciprocity depends on the means. Sudama corroborates both viewpoints -- the size of the favor depends on the means and intention; the size of the reciprocity depends on the means of the reciprocator. 
    • Chanakya Nīti Sūtra 6.32,
        ◦ स्वल्पोपकारकृतेऽपि प्रत्युपकारं कर्तुमार्यो जागर्ति ॥ 
        ◦ A noble person is ever ready to reciprocate, even for a small benefaction. 
        ◦ Earlier in 6.29
            ▪ तिलमात्रमप्युपकारं शैलमात्रं मन्यते साधुः ॥
            ▪ A virtuous person regards even a benefaction as small as a sesame seed to be as great as a mountain.
        ◦ Read together they convey the kural’s idea that the reciprocation depends on the character of the recipient not the size of the original benefaction. 
    • Śrīharṣa's Naiṣadhīyacarita, Sarga II, verse 14 
        ◦ अचिरादुपकर्तुराचरेदथवात्मौपयिकीमुपक्रियाम् । पृथुरित्थमथाणुरस्तु सा न विशेष विदुषामिह ग्रहः ॥
        ◦ It occurs in the episode of the swan's gratitude to Nala, and its language comes unusually close to the distinction we are investigating.
        ◦ The preceding verse has the swan acknowledge that, although it is only a bird and cannot adequately repay Nala, it is nevertheless impelled to return the benefit. Verse 14 then says that one should promptly perform a reciprocal service to one's benefactor, suited to one's own means; whether that service is great or small is not a matter on which the wise insist. 
        ◦ This illuminates செயப்பட்டார் சால்பின் வரைத்து from the perspective of the reciprocator's capacity and disposition. The size of the return is not an absolute measure of gratitude. 
    • Ramayana 4.27.45
        ◦ उपकारेण वीरस्तु प्रतिकारेण युज्यते । अकृतज्ञोऽप्रतिकृतो हन्ति सत्त्ववतां मनः ॥
        ◦ The idea is that a virtuous person responds to assistance with reciprocal assistance; failure to reciprocate wounds the hearts of the virtuous.
        ◦ उपकारेण वीरस्तु प्रतिकारेण युज्यते matches the உதவி வரைத்தன் றுதவி யுதவி, but the cheyyappattar salbu is only obliquely referenced in the negative case where the ingratitude and non-reciprocity is condemned. 
    • The Sudāmā episode in the *Śrīmad Bhāgavata* (10.81.3, 6–7) offers an illuminating narrative corroboration. Kṛṣṇa asks Sudāmā what gift he has brought and declares: **किमुपायनमानीतं ब्रह्मन्मे भवता गृहात् । अण्वप्युपाहृतं भक्तैः प्रेम्णा भूर्येव मे भवेत् । भूर्यप्यभक्तोपहृतं न मे तोषाय कल्पते ॥** (10.81.3)—even the smallest offering, when presented with love by a devotee, becomes great to Him, whereas even a substantial offering made without devotion does not please Him. Sudāmā's humble offering of flattened rice, made not from any desire for material wealth but out of affection, thus reveals his own disposition. Kṛṣṇa, recognising this, understands that Sudāmā has never approached Him seeking prosperity and has now come out of affection for his devoted wife: सर्वभूतात्मदृक् साक्षात् तस्यागमनकारणम् । ज्ञायाचिन्तयन्नायं श्रीकामो माभजत्पुरा ॥ पत्न्याः पतिव्रतायास्तु सखा प्रियचिकीर्षया । प्राप्तो मामस्य दास्यामि सम्पदोऽमर्त्यदुर्लभाः ॥. The important part is **श्रीकामो नाभजत्पूर्वं ...  प्राप्तो मामस्य दास्यामि सम्पदोऽमर्त्यदुर्लभाः. Krishna resolves to bestow upon him riches beyond the reach of the immortals. The smallness of Sudāmā's offering and the immensity of Kṛṣṇa's reciprocation are thus not measures of material exchange, but expressions of their respective natures. This illuminates Valluvar's **உதவி வரைத்தன்று உதவி**: the return is not bounded by the magnitude of the original benefit, but is **செயப்பட்டார் சால்பின் வரைத்து**—measured by the character of the one who reciprocates. This episode beautifully brings both dimensions together. 
        ◦ Sudāma's offering is materially small, but his love and absence of material expectation reveal the quality of his disposition.
        ◦ Krishna recognises this and resolves to bestow immense wealth, revealing the boundless generosity of the reciprocator.

        ◦ त्यज दुर्जनसंसर्गं भज साधुसमागमम् । कुरु पुण्यमहोरात्र स्मर नित्यमनित्यताम् ॥ Abandon the company of the wicked; cultivate the association of the good; perform meritorious deeds day and night; constantly remember impermanence.
        ◦ आपत्सु मित्रं जानीयाद्रणे शूरं रहः शुचिम् । भार्यां च विभवे क्षीणे दुर्भिक्षे च प्रियातिथिम् ॥ One should recognise a friend in adversity, a warrior in battle, a pure person in private conduct, a wife when prosperity has disappeared, and a welcome guest during famine.
        ◦ The first verse supplies the positive injunction to cultivate the company of the good, भज साधुसमागमम्, corresponding to மறவற்க மாசற்றார் கேண்மை. Its preceding injunction, त्यज दुर्जनसंसर्गम्, and the expression स्मर नित्यम् further resonate with Valluvar's use of துற and மறவற்க, although the object of स्मर in the Sanskrit verse is impermanence, not virtuous association. The second verse complements this by enjoining recognition of a true friend in adversity, आपत्सु मित्रं जानीयात्; the quality which Valluvar expresses as a direct injunction not to forsake the friendship of those who have stood by us in distress, துறவற்க துன்பத்துட் டுப்பாயார் நட்பு. The expression शुचिम्, describing the person whose purity is tested in private conduct, provides a further lexical resonance with மாசற்றார், while भार्यां illustrates the broader treatment of relationships and their reliability under changing circumstances, relevant to the wider sense of கேண்மை.
        ◦ The surrounding verses reinforce the correspondence. Garuda Purana 1.108.11: उत्तमैः सह साङ्गत्यं पण्डितैः सह सत्कथाम् । अलुब्धैः सह मित्रत्वं कुर्वाणो नावसीदति ॥ One who cultivates association with excellent people, discussion with the learned, and friendship with the ungreedy does not come to grief.  उत्तमैः सह साङ्गत्यम्, association with excellent people, and अलुब्धैः सह मित्रत्वं friendship with the ungreedy directly resonate with the kural. Verses 1.108.13–14 distinguish genuine relationships from mere kinship, declaring स बन्धुर्यो हिते युक्तः — he is the true relative who is devoted to one's welfare.  The verses reinforce மறவற்க in multiple senses each of மாசற்றார் and கேண்மை. Garuda Purana 1.109.6: अर्थेन किं कृपणहस्तगतेन केन ज्ञानेन किं बहुशठाग्रहसंकुलेन । रूपेण किं गुणपराक्रमवर्जितेन मित्रेण किं व्यसनकालपराङ्मुखेन ॥ What is the use of wealth in the hands of a miser, knowledge accompanied by deceit, or beauty devoid of virtue and prowess? What is the use of a friend who turns away when adversity comes?. This verse asks rhetorically मित्रेण किं व्यसनकालपराङ्मुखेन — what value is a friend who turns away in times of adversity, which Valluvar expresses as a direct injunction: துறவற்க துன்பத்துட் டுப்பாயார் நட்பு. 
        ◦ The two chapters 1.108 and 1.109 offer a coherent ethical framework: cultivate the company of virtuous people, distinguish genuine relationships from merely nominal ones, and recognise the true friend through steadfastness in adversity. This combination captures the two complementary dimensions of Valluvar's kural, even though the Sanskrit passages do not reproduce its precise formulation.

    • Valmiki Ramayana 4.33.10
        ◦ पूर्वं कृतार्थो मित्राणां न तत् प्रतिकरोति यः । कृतघ्नः सर्वभूतानां स वध्यः प्लवगेश्वर ॥
        ◦ A person who has previously received benefits from friends but does not reciprocate them is ungrateful and deserves to be slain, O lord of the Vanaras.
        ◦ This verse is interesting for these reasons: 
            ▪ पूर्वं कृतार्थः introduces the idea of a person who has previously received a benefit. This resonates with ஒன்றுநன்று உள்ள, but the Ramayana is concerned with reciprocating that benefit rather than remembering it in the face of subsequent injury.
            ▪ स वध्यः introduces the idea that an ungrateful person deserves to be slain. This is an interesting lexical resonance with கொன்றன்ன, but the two verses use the idea of killing in entirely different ways. Valluvar describes the magnitude of the injury; Valmiki describes the punishment deserved by the ungrateful person.
            ▪ This verse belongs to Sugriva's confrontation with his failure to fulfil his obligation to Rama. It is part of the same larger admonition that culminates in Kishkindha Kanda 4.33.12 that parallels the next kural (எந்நன்றி கொன்றார்க்கு) closely.

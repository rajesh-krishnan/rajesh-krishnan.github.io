## Verse 2

| Kural | Shloka |
|-------|--------|
| கற்றதனால் ஆய பயனென்கொல் வாலறிவன் <br> நற்றாள் தொழாஅர் எனின். | अविज्ञाते परे तत्त्वे शास्त्राधीतिस्तु निष्फला \| <br> विज्ञातेऽपि परे तत्त्वे शास्त्राधीतिस्तु निष्फला \|\| <br> Vivekacudamani 59 <br> रेखामयध्वजसुधाकलशातपत्र-वज्राङ्कुशाम्बुरुहकल्पकशङ्खचक्रैः \| <br> भव्यैरलङ्कृततलौ परतत्त्वचिह्नैः श्रीवेङ्कटेशचरणौ शरणं प्रपद्ये \|\| <br> Venkateshvara Prapatti 5 |

### Comments and Research Notes

The kural rhetorically questions the use of learning if it does not lead to the feet of the One with Supreme Knowledge. A recurrent theme in this Adhikaram is the juxtaposition of a strong jnana yoga proposition with a strong bhakti proposition.

The match is provided by a pair of shlokas from different sources, using परतत्त्व as the unifying anchor. The first shloka from Vivekacudamani by Adi Shankaracharya, who declares that when the Supreme Reality is not known, knowledge of the shastras is fruitless. Thus शास्त्राधीतिस्तु निष्फला. corresponds to the rhetorical question கற்றதனால் ஆய பயனென்கொல். The shloka fuirther adds that the knowledge of the shastras is fruitless even when one realizes the Supreme Relaity. So where is this परतत्त्व to be found? 

The second shloka declares surrender to the soles of the Lord (in the name and form of Venkateshvara in this case), whose soles are adorned by emblems of the Supreme Reality. The Lord is by extension the locus of Supreme Reality. Thus परतत्त्वचिह्नैः श्रीवेङ्कटेशचरणौ शरणं correponds to வாலறிவன் நற்றாள் தொழ.
 
Note that some versions of the Vivekacudamani list the shloka as Verse 61.

The same idea is also captured in first verse of Adi Shankaracharya’ Bhaja Govindam: भज गोविन्दं भज गोविन्दं गोविन्दं भज मूढमते । सम्प्राप्ते सन्निहिते काले नहि नहि रक्षति डुकृङ्करणे ॥ This verse points out the futility of learning the advanced rules of grammar, as that will not save one when one approaches dearh; instead, it urges worhsipping the Lord (in the name and form of Govinda).

While this connects well  to the futility of lerning கற்றதனால் ஆய பயனென்கொல் without worship of the Lord, the connection to வாலறிவன் is subtle. In his commentary on the Vishnu Sahasranama, for the word Govinda, Adi Shankara cites traditional etymology (Nirukta) : गोभिर्वाणीभिः विन्दते, वेत्ति वेदान्तवाक्यैरिति वा गोविन्दः। ‘गोभिरेव यतो वेद्यो गोविन्दः समुदाहृतः’इति श्रीविष्णुतिलके।

I interpret வாலறிவன் as an epithet of the Lord, since this kural is in the chapter praising God. If we interpret the word as a Guru with Supreme Knowledge, then Verse 154 of the Guru Gita makes the connection: गुरुर्ब्रह्मा गुरुर्विष्णुर्गुरुर्देवो महेश्वरः । गुरुः साक्षात् परं ब्रह्म तस्मै श्रीगुरवे नमः ॥

### Verse 7

The first line of the Hitopadesha verse is a good semantic match for the kural. இவன்முடிக்கும் packs a punch that कुशलः does not. The Sanskrit word indicates capability/skill, whereas the Tamil word emphasizes competent task completion. The second line of the Hitopadesha verse clarifies that someone with mere textbook knowledge and not experience should not be appointed, which the kural does not elaborate, but subtly hints with இவன்முடிக்கும். While both stress the need for evaluating competence for the task, the kural additionally highlights the need to also evaluate the means to be employed.
 
Another candidate I had considered was परीक्षितगुणान् नित्यं प्रौढभावान् धुरंधरान् । पञ्चोपधाव्यतीतांश्च कुर्याद् राजार्थकारिणः ॥ from the Mahabharata Shanti Parva verse 12.84.20. It does incorporate notions of task assignment after evaluation of competence, and the word धुरंधर packs more of a punch than कुशलः. The focus of the verse is really on how to evaluate a person's competence, rather than the task and means of accomplishment.The evaluation here includes tests of character, maturity, competence, and resistance to five temptations (धर्म, अर्थ, काम, भय, and लोभ). 

There are numerous Sanskrit texts that discuss the evaluation of appropriate means (upāya), but I could not find one that combines the task, means, and competent task completion aspects in a single verse.

    • Arthashastra 1.19.34:
        ◦ प्रजासुखे सुखं राज्ञः प्रजानां च हिते हितम् । नात्मप्रियं हितं राज्ञः प्रजानां तु प्रियं हितम् ॥
        ◦ In the happiness of the subjects lies the king’s happiness; in their welfare lies his welfare. What pleases the king personally is not necessarily his good; the people’s good is his good.
    • Discernment and *நன்மை பயக்கும்*: a tripartite reading of the Tirukkural
        ◦ Across the three Pāls, Valluvar may be read as applying a common principle of discernment: **distinguish between alternatives and judge them by the good they bring about, rather than treating their apparent opposites as invariably good or bad.**
            ▪ **Aṟattuppāl:** *இருமை வகைதெரிந்து* (Kural 23) expresses discernment between alternatives in the pursuit of virtue. Yet even here, *பொய்மையும் வாய்மை யிடத்த புரைதீர்ந்த நன்மை பயக்கும் எனின்* (Kural 292) shows that an apparently absolute distinction between truth and falsehood may be qualified when falsehood yields faultless good. The operative criterion is *நன்மை பயக்கும்*.
            ▪ **Poruṭpāl:** *நன்மையுந் தீமையுநாடி நலம்புரிந்த தன்மையான்* applies this discernment to governance. The ruler must examine the consequences of possible courses and favour what serves welfare (Arthashastra 1.19.34 *प्रजानां हितम्*), with judgement attentive to circumstances rather than confined to rigid labels. For example, the king may employ deception and espionage in statecraft for the welfare of his people. Thus, நலம்புரிந்த points to a disposition oriented toward beneficial outcomes, rather than an inflexible classification of every possible means as inherently good or bad.
            ▪ **Kāmattuppāl:** the same interpretive lens may be extended, speculatively, to *ஊடல்* and *கூடல்*. Their apparent opposition is not absolute: sulking or feigned anger may itself deepen affection, while reconciliation brings a different pleasure. Their value depends on how they function within the relationship.  இருமை வகைதெரிந்து of Aṟattuppāl manifests as discerning the duality of ஊடல் and கூடல்; நன்மையுந் தீமையுநாடி of Poruṭpāl manifests as weighing the respective pleasures and drawbacks of ஊடுதல் காமத்திற் கின்பம் and இன்பங் கூடி முயங்கப் பெறின்; and நலம்புரிந்த தன்மை manifests as discerning வல்ல தவரளிக்கும் ஆறு—the way the capable lover responds. 
        ◦ The *Kaṭha Upaniṣad*’s distinction between *śreyas* and *preyas* (1.2.2) can serve as a broad conceptual corroboration, provided *śreyas* is not simplistically equated with moral good and *preyas* with evil. The point is discerning what is genuinely beneficial rather than merely attractive in the immediate moment.
        ◦ Thus, across *Aṟam*, *Poruḷ*, and *Kāmam*, the recurring principle may be stated as: **discern the alternatives, consider their consequences in context, and favour what truly brings about *நன்மை*.** This is a synthetic interpretation of Valluvar’s thought, not a claim that every occurrence of *நன்மை*, *தீமை*, *அறம்*, or *śreyas/preyas* has precisely the same meaning in every context.

The chapter’s conceptual skeleton may emerge most clearly from reading the Arthaśāstra alongside the Pañcatantra–Hitopadeśa tradition, while the other Nīti,  Dharma, and Purana texts provide parallel formulations, corroboration, and sometimes more specific treatments of individual ideas.
There is a productive division of labour between these bodies of literature:
    • Arthaśāstra: systematic analysis of administrative decisions, personnel, resources, counsel, and task accomplishment.
    • Pañcatantra and Hitopadeśa: compact, memorable formulations of practical wisdom about people, loyalty, competence, and the consequences of misjudgment.
    • Kāmandakīya Nītisāra: a related theoretical treatment of statecraft and political methods.
    • Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa: broader reflections on royal responsibility, personnel, vigilance, and the welfare of the realm.
    • Yājñavalkya Smṛti, Matsya Purāṇa, Viṣṇudharmottara Purāṇa, and other texts: potentially valuable parallels for appointment criteria, task-specific competence, and the proper administration of work.

## Comments and Research Notes

## Verse 1

I propose Mahabharata, Striparva 11.12.4 (BORI) as the primary parallel for this kural. The correspondence lies in दोषानवेक्षते (examines faults) with தீமையு நாடி, while परं श्रेयः स विन्दति (attains the highest good) closely echoes நலம்புரிந்த. The examination of நன்மை is implicit in the movement from scrutinizing faults to discerning and attaining श्रेयस्; राजा provides the explicit context of rulership for ஆளப் படும். The context makes this implicit dimension particularly significant: earlier, in 11.12.2, Krishna reminds Dhritarashtra of his learning in the Vedas, Shastras, and Rajadharma—श्रुतानि च पुराणानि राजधर्माश्च—so discerning the good is a standard he already knows but has failed to apply. The surrounding rebuke makes the connection especially pointed: Dhritarashtra has failed to examine his own misconduct and its consequences for the Pandavas’ rightful claim. Krishna’s injunction in 11.12.6, ततोऽन्यवृत्तमात्मानं समवेक्षस्व (examine yourself as one who has acted otherwise than properly), reinforces the demand for self-scrutiny.

The broader ethical principle is expressed in Kathopanishad 1.2.2: श्रेयश्च प्रेयश्च मनुष्यमेतस्तौ सम्परीत्य विविनक्ति धीरः \| श्रेयो हि धीरोऽभिप्रेयसो वृणीते प्रेयो मन्दो योगक्षेमाद्वृणीते \|\|. The good and the pleasant approach a person. Having examined them, the discerning person distinguishes between the two. The wise choose the good over the pleasant, whereas the less discerning choose the pleasant for the sake of acquisition and preservation. सम्परीत्य, having examined thoroughly, illuminates நாடி; श्रेयस् and प्रेयस् offer a conceptual lens for நன்மை and தீமை, although the latter pair is not an exact lexical equivalent of the good and the pleasant. The choice of श्रेयस् over प्रेयस् also clarifies நலம்புரிந்த: the orientation is toward what is genuinely beneficial, rather than what is merely attractive or immediately advantageous.

Within the specific domain of statecraft, Arthashastra 1.19.34 establishes the governing criterion: प्रजासुखे सुखं राज्ञः प्रजानां च हिते हितम् \| नात्मप्रियं हितं राज्ञः प्रजानां तु प्रियं हितम् \|\|. In the happiness of the subjects lies the king’s happiness; in their welfare lies his welfare. What pleases the king personally is not necessarily his good; the subjects’ good is his good. This supplies the political dimension of the distinction between immediate preference and genuine welfare: the ruler’s judgment must be directed toward the good of those he governs, not merely toward what pleases him. Read together, the three sources distinguish the act of examining alternatives, the ethical criterion by which they are judged, and the public welfare that should govern the ruler’s choice.


### Verse 2

I propose Kautilya's Arthashastra 2.6.28 as the primary parallel for this kural. Valluvar’s வாரி பெருக்கி corresponds to समुदयं वृद्धिं, while வளம்படுத்து is reflected in the combined injunction चायस्य दर्शयेत् ह्रासं व्ययस्य—to show an increase in revenue and reduce expenditure. The உற்றவை யாராய்வான் is consistent with प्राज्ञः साधयेच्च विपर्ययम्, which emphasizes the capable administrator’s responsibility to prevent the reversal of these gains. The செய்க வினை corresponds to एवं कुर्यात्.

Arthashastra 2.9.36 offers a further corroboration: न भक्षयन्ति ये त्वर्थान्न्यायतो वर्धयन्ति च नित्याधिकाराः कार्यास्ते राज्ञः प्रियहिते रताः \|\|. The correspondence வாரி பெருக்கி = अर्थान् … वर्धयन्ति links revenue enhancement with the officials’ duty to increase the king’s resources, while செய்க வினை = नित्याधिकाराः कार्याः adds the dimension of retaining such officials in permanent service. The phrase न भक्षयन्ति ये त्वर्थान् further brings out the avoidance of waste, fraud, and abuse of public resources, which is one, though not the only, aspect of வளம்படுத்து.

The financial focus is corroborated by Yajnavalkya Smriti 1.322: तत्र तत्र च निष्णातानध्यक्षान् कुशलान् शुचीन् \| प्रकुर्यादायकर्मान्तव्ययकर्मसु चोद्यतान् \|\| — superintendents should be appointed who are expert in their respective fields, competent, upright, and diligent in matters of revenue and expenditure. The passage thus places the kural’s concern with financial growth and prudent expenditure within the broader principle of assigning responsibilities to those with the appropriate expertise. The Mahabharata, Shanti Parva (BORI) 12.120.21, similarly generalizes this principle: क्षुद्रं क्रूरं तथा प्राज्ञं शूरं चार्थविशारदम् \| स्वकर्मणि नियुञ्जीत ये चान्ये वचनाधिकाः \|\| — each person should be assigned to their proper work, including those skilled in wealth and finance (अर्थविशारदम्).


### Verse 3

I propose Arthashastra 1.8.26 as the primary parallel for this kural. Kautilya recommends appointing ministers on the basis of qualities including अभिजन, प्रज्ञा, शौच, शौर्य, and अनुराग (good lineage, intelligence, purity, courage, and loyalty), with appointments based on the pre-eminence of their qualities: गुणप्राधान्यात्. Parimelazhakar glosses the four qualities specified by Valluvar as “அரசன் மாட்டு அன்பும், அவனுக்கு ஆவன அறியும் அறிவும், அவை செய்தற்கண் கலங்காமையும், அவற்றால் பொருள் கையுற்ற வழி அதன்மேல் அவா இன்மையும் ஆகிய”—love for the king, knowledge of what benefits him, freedom from perturbation in carrying out his purposes, and absence of desire for the wealth acquired through those actions. These provide the interpretive basis for mapping Valluvar’s four qualities to Kautilya’s list:

  - அன்பு — अनुराग -- as अनुराग denotes both affection and loyalty, it closely matches Parimelazhakar’s explanation of அன்பு as love for the king.
  - அறிவு — प्रज्ञा -- This is a direct correspondence: both denote intelligence or sound understanding.
  - அவாவின்மை — शौच -- शौच denotes purity or cleanliness of thought, word, and deed. Valluvar’s own treatment supports this mapping. Kural 34 states: மனத்துக்கண் மாசிலன் ஆதல் அனைத்தறன் ஆகுல நீர பிற (Freedom from blemish in the mind is the entirety of virtue; all else is mere appearance) and Kural 35 further specifies: அழுக்காறு அவாவெகுளி இன்னாச்சொல் நான்கும் இழுக்கா இயன்றது அறம் (Virtue consists in avoiding these four: envy, desire, anger, and hurtful speech). Together, these verses show how freedom from mental blemish (மாசின்மை) entails avoiding corrupting dispositions, including desire (அவா). This supports mapping அவாவின்மை to शौच, which encompasses freedom from desire within a broader ideal of purity of thought, speech, and action. Kautilya’s criterion is thus broader than Valluvar’s specific quality.
  - தேற்றம் — शौर्य -- We read தேற்றம் primarily as courage and determination in the face of obstacles, consistent with Parimelazhakar’s gloss கலங்காமை, remaining unperturbed while carrying out the assigned purposes. This maps naturally to शौर्य, courage or valour. Since தேற்றம் has a wider semantic range, including other possible senses relevant to ministerial selection, we return to it below.

Thus, இந்நான்கு நன்குடையான் கட்டே தெளிவு identifies possession of all four qualities as the basis for confidence in a minister’s suitability, while Kautilya likewise prescribes appointing ministers endowed with the listed qualities, giving precedence to their pre-eminence: युक्तानमात्यान् कुर्वीत गुणप्राधान्यात्. Kautilya’s list additionally includes अभिजन, good lineage, which Valluvar does not specify among these four qualities. The two lists therefore converge on key personal qualifications for office while differing in scope and emphasis.

#### Additional Corroborations

Kamandakiya Nitisara IV.24 corroborates Valluvar’s list of qualities: कुलीनाः शुचयः शूराः श्रुतवन्तोऽनुरागिणः \| दण्डनीतेः प्रयोक्तारः सचिवाः स्युर्महीपतेः \|\|. The verse states that the king’s ministers should be well-born, pure, brave, learned, loyal, and capable of applying statecraft. श्रुतवन्तः, learned or well-versed, complements அறிவு; शुचयः corresponds to அவாவின்மை through the broader ideal of purity; शूराः supports தேற்றம் as courage; and अनुरागिणः corresponds to அன்பு as loyalty and attachment. The inclusion of lineage and expertise in applying statecraft extends beyond Valluvar’s four qualities, but accords with the broader Sanskrit tradition.

Mahabharata, Shanti Parva 12.84.19 (BORI), offers a longer list: येषां वैनयिकी बुद्धिः प्रकृता चैव शोभना \| तेजो धैर्यं क्षमा शौचमनुराग स्थितिर्धृतिः \|\|. It includes cultivated intelligence, energy, fortitude, forbearance, purity, loyalty, steadiness, and firmness. The Mahabharata list contains close counterparts to all four of Valluvar’s qualities: अनुराग corresponds to அன்பு, as affection and loyalty; वैनयिकी बुद्धिः, cultivated intelligence shaped by learning and humility, complements அறிவு; धैर्यं, स्थितिः, and धृतिः reinforce various aspects of தேற்றம் as mental fortitude and steadfastness; and शौच corresponds to அவாவின்மை within the broader ideal of purity. 
The following verse, 12.84.20, further corroborates this emphasis on discernment through examination: परीक्षितगुणान्नित्यं प्रौढभावान्धुरंधरान् \| पञ्चोपधाव्यतीतांश्च कुर्याद्राजार्थकारिणः \|\|. The phrase परीक्षितगुणान् (‘those whose qualities have been examined’) reinforces the sense of தெளிவு as a considered judgment of suitability, while कुर्याद्राजार्थकारिणः (appoint ministers who act for the king’s purposes) connects that judgment to the selection of ministers.

Yajnavalkya Smriti 1.322 further attests to शुचीन् (upright, pure) as a qualification for officials entrusted with financial administration. The full verse reads: तत्र तत्र च निष्णातानध्यक्षान् कुशलान् शुचीन् \| प्रकुर्यादायकर्मान्तव्ययकर्मसु चोद्यतान् \|\|. It recommends appointing experts in their respective fields who are skilled, upright, and diligent in revenue and expenditure administration. 

Thus, both Valluvar and the Sanskrit statecraft tradition treat purity of character—including freedom from acquisitive desire and incorruptibility—as an important qualification for appointment to office.

#### An alternative reading of தேற்றம்

The term தேற்றம் may also be considered in the sense of discernment or assurance reached through examination. The Sanskrit statecraft tradition gives considerable attention to the testing of prospective ministers through उपधा. Kamandakiya Nitisara IV.26 defines this procedure: उपेत्य धीयते यस्मादुपधेति ततः स्मृता \| उपाय उपधा ज्ञेया तयामात्यान् परीक्षयेत् \|\|. The verse explains the term upadha and prescribes its use in examining ministers. Note that the Mahabharata, Shanti Parva 12.84.20 (BORI), recommends appointing five (ministers, according to Nilakantha's gloss) who have passed the upadhas (पञ्चोपधाव्यतीतांश्च), but does not specify the number or nature of the upadhas themselves. Arthashastra 1.10.1-20 describes four upadhas—धर्मोपधा (dharma), अर्थोपधा (wealth), कामोपधा (desire), and भयोपधा (fear)—through which the purity or impurity of prospective ministers is ascertained: उपधाभिः शौचाशौचज्ञानममात्यानाम् (through the upadhas, one learns the purity or impurity of ministers). This explicit connection between उपधा and शौच strengthens the proposed link to Valluvar’s அவாவின்மை, understood as freedom from desire within the broader ideal of purity of character. 

I therefore favor reading தேற்றம் primarily as mental fortitude, steadiness, or freedom from inner perturbation, corresponding to Kautilya's शौर्य and reinforced by the Mahabharata's धैर्य, स्थितिः, and धृतिः. The Upadha material remains a useful interpretive possibility, but the direct correspondence with the established list of ministerial qualities is stronger. More broadly, the Sanskrit sources attest a shared repertoire of qualifications, often adding lineage and other attributes beyond Valluvar’s four. 
The lists thus converge in principle while differing in scope and emphasis.


### Verse 4

I propose Matsya Purana 214.44–46 as the primary parallel for this kural. The opening verse states उत्तमाधममध्यानि बुद्ध्वा कर्माणि पार्थिवः \| उत्तमाधममध्येषु पुरुषेषु नियोजयेत् \|\|. The verse says that, having understood the superior, inferior, and intermediate kinds of work, the king should assign them to superior, inferior, and intermediate persons. This directly corresponds to வினைவகையான் வேறாகு மாந்தர் பலர்: people differ in their suitability for different kinds of work. The subsequent verses specify the criteria for assessing individuals—नियोगं पौरुषं भक्तिं श्रुतं शौर्यं कुलं नयम् (appointment, capability, loyalty, learning, valor, lineage, and practical judgment). These details illuminate the several ways people have been assessed, எனைவகையாற் றேறியக் கண்ணும்; the Matsya Purana list shows that assessment considers several dimensions, but even such assessment does not eliminate differences in task-specific suitability. The expression पुरुषान्तरविज्ञानतत्त्वसारनिबन्धनात् further emphasizes that such decisions depend on genuine knowledge of individual differences.

The Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda 2.94.20, gives a concise parallel to the matching of tasks and personnel: कच्चिन्मुख्या महत्स्वेव मध्यमेषु च मध्यमाः \| जघन्याश्च जघन्येषु भृत्याः कर्मसु योजिताः \|\|, that is, are your foremost servants assigned to the most important tasks, the middling ones to middling tasks, and the least capable to lesser tasks. This directly corroborates the Matsya Purana’s उत्तमाधममध्यानि … कर्माणि and illustrates one dimension of வினைவகையான் வேறாகு மாந்தர்: assignments should reflect the relative importance of the work and the capabilities of those undertaking it.

The kural’s வினைவகையான் வேறாகு can also encompass differences in people’s propensities and skills across kinds of work. The Mahabharata Shanti Parva, 12.120.21 (BORI Critical Edition), recommends assigning people according to their particular qualities: क्षुद्रं क्रूरं तथा प्राज्ञं शूरं चार्थविशारदम् \| स्वकर्मणि नियुञ्जीत ये चान्ये वचनाधिकाः \|\|, that is, he should assign each person to the appropriate work, whether petty, harsh, wise, valorous, or skilled in wealth and finance, as well as those distinguished in counsel and speech.

The Arthashastra 2.9.3 adds a distinct nuance: अश्वसधर्माणो हि मनुष्या नियुक्ताः कर्मसु विकुर्वते \|\|, that is human beings, like horses, behave differently when appointed to tasks. This corroborates வினைவகையான் வேறாகு மாந்தர் from the perspective of actual performance: people exhibit differences when engaged in work. The comparison with horses suggests a further consideration: assessment alone may not settle the question, since people’s conduct can reveal additional differences once they are assigned to a task.

Thus, the Matsya Purana and Ramayana emphasize matching the level of work to the capability of the person; the Mahabharata illustrates assignment according to particular skills and qualities; and the Arthashastra highlights differences that emerge in actual work performance. Together, these sources illuminate the kural’s distinction between assessing people for selection and discerning their suitability for particular tasks.


### Verse 5

I propose Hitopadesha 3.56 as the primary parallel for this kural. The opening line, यो यत्र कुशलः कार्ये तं तत्र विनियोजयेत्, corresponds to அறிந்தாற்றிச் செய்கிற்பாற் … வினைதான் … ஏவற்பால்: one should assign a task to a person competent in that particular work. Valluvar’s சிறந்தான் corresponds to शास्त्रज्ञः, the person distinguished by learning, while அல்லால் explicitly rejects such distinction alone as sufficient grounds for assignment. The Hitopadesha supplies the rationale in its second line, अदृष्ट-कर्मा यः शास्त्रज्ञोऽपि विमुह्यति: even a learned person may falter when inexperienced in practical work. Valluvar explicitly rejects distinction alone as sufficient grounds for assignment, while the Hitopadesha explains why: even a learned person may falter when undertaking work in which he lacks practical experience.

The Arthashastra 1.8.25 corroborates the Hitopadesha’s rationale for the kural’s restriction: शास्त्रविददृष्टकर्मा कर्मसु विषादं गच्छेत् \|\|—one who knows the treatises but lacks practical experience may falter in work.

Shukraniti 2.115 offers a limited parallel: तत्कार्ये कुशलं चान्यं तत्पदानुगतं खलु \| नियोजयेद्वर्तने तु तदभावे तथा परम् \|\|. The verse says, appoint another who is skilled in that particular task and suited to the position; in that person’s absence, appoint another likewise. This supports அறிந்தாற்றிச் செய்கிற்பாற் கல்லால், which makes task-specific competence the condition for assignment. The following verse adds that even a son may be appointed only if he possesses the requisite qualities: तद्गुणो यदि तत्पुत्रः (if his son possesses those same qualities). The surrounding discussion places task-specific appointment within a broader administrative strategy of rotating officials to prevent the accumulation and abuse of power. Śukranīti thus extends the principle to qualified substitution, rotation, and merit-based succession.


### Verse 6

I propose Kautilya's Arthashastra 1.8.29 as the primary parallel for this kural. Valluvar’s செய்வானை நாடி வினைநாடி finds a close correspondence in Kautilya’s instruction to assess capacity when distributing responsibilities, सामर्थ्यश्च विभज्यामात्यविभवं, and to consider the work itself, कर्म च; காலத்தோ டெய்த is echoed in देशकालौ च, while செயல் finds its counterpart in कार्याः. The scope is not confined to appointing counsellors: Kautilya explicitly extends the principle to all such officials, सर्व एवैताः कार्याः, making the suitability of the person to the task, rather than general distinction alone, central to the correspondence.

The देशकाल motif is corroborated in the Mahabharata, Shanti Parva 12.84.22 (BORI Critical Edition): देशकालविधानज्ञान्भर्तृकार्यहितैषिणः \| नित्यमर्थेषु सर्वेषु राजा कुर्वीत मन्त्रिणः \|\| — the king should appoint as ministers those who understand place, time, and procedure, and who seek the interests of their master’s work in all affairs. Valluvar’s காலத்தோ டெய்த should likewise be read in the broader context of இடம்–பொருள்–ஏவல்: although இடம் remains implicit in the Kural, Sanskrit niti texts repeatedly couple place and time as देशकाल.

The broader logic of successful execution is made explicit in Arthashastra 1.15.42: कर्मणामारम्भोपायः पुरुषद्रव्यसम्पद्देशकालविभागो विनिपातप्रतीकारः कार्यसिद्धिरिति पञ्चाङ्गो मन्त्रः \|. Kautilya’s framework connects the means of initiating a task, the personnel and material available, the allocation according to place and time, and remedies for contingencies with successful completion; correspondingly, पुरुष resonates with செய்வானை, देशकालविभाग with காலத்தோ டெய்த, and कर्मणामारम्भोपायः together with कार्यसिद्धिः with Valluvar’s வினைநாடி … செயல்.


### Verse 7

I propose Arthashastra 1.15.42 as the primary parallel for this kural. Valluvar’s இதனை is the task to be accomplished, corresponding to कर्मणाम्; இதனால் encompasses the methods, personnel, material, and wealth required for its execution, reflected in आरम्भोपायः and पुरुषद्रव्यसम्पत्; and இவன் முடிக்கும் corresponds to the assessment of the individual’s capacity to accomplish the task, culminating in कार्यसिद्धिः. The instruction to entrust the task to him, அதனை அவன்கண் விடல், follows from this assessment, while என்றாய்ந்து corresponds to the deliberative process expressed in Kautilya’s concluding definition: इति पञ्चाङ्गो मन्त्रः.  There is substantial verbal as well as conceptual correspondence, though the two passages differ in scope: Kautilya enumerates the five aspects of counsel—methods, personnel and resources, allocation by place and time, remedies for setbacks, and successful completion—while Valluvar distils the deliberation into the task, the means and resources required, the individual’s capacity, and the decision to delegate.

The correspondence of இதனால் with Kautilya’s आरम्भोपायः and पुरुषद्रव्यसम्पत् also invites a more specific consideration of upaya (means) in its political sense. The Arthashastra defines these measures at 2.10.47: उपायाः सामोपप्रदानभेददण्डाः \|\|. The upaya are conciliation, inducement, division, and coercion. These four upayas illustrate the methods available to a ruler, while पुरुषद्रव्यसम्पत् extends the assessment to personnel, material, and wealth required for execution. Together, the passages illuminate the breadth of Valluvar’s compact இதனால் without restricting it to the four political measures.

A further corroboration is available in the Vishnudharmottara Purana 2.24.10, which describes the qualifications of a military commander: कृतज्ञाः कर्मणां शूरास्तथा क्लेशसहा ऋजुः \| व्यूहतत्त्वविधानज्ञः फल्गुसारविशेषवित् \|\|. The qualities शूराः, क्लेशसहाः, and ऋजुः—courage, endurance under difficulty, and uprightness—bear on whether இவன் முடிக்கும்; व्यूहतत्त्वविधानज्ञः, knowledge of the principles and deployment of formations, concerns the means by which the task is performed; and फल्गुसारविशेषवित्, the ability to distinguish weakness from strength, adds a dimension of discriminative judgment. The same chapter’s preceding verses 3–7 address the selection and assignment of royal aides more directly, including यथायोग्येषु भार्गव (according to suitability).


### Verse 8

I propose Matsya Purana 215.83 as the primary parallel for this kural. The expression यस्मिन् कर्मणि यस्य स्याद्विशेषेण च कौशलम् not only corresponds to வினைக்குரிமை but also defines the basis of that fitness: the person’s particular skill in the specific work under consideration. The word परीक्ष्य directly corresponds to நாடிய பின்றை, indicating that the candidate is to be examined before the appointment is made. Finally, तस्मिन् कर्मणि तं … विनिवेशयेत् corresponds to அதற்குரிய னாகச் செயல்: having identified and examined the person’s task-specific competence, the king should assign him to that very work. The Sanskrit makes the criterion of suitability explicit through the notion of specialized skill, whereas Valluvar expresses it through வினைக்குரிமை—fitness for the task. In essence, both passages prescribe assigning work on the basis of criteria specific to that work, implicitly distinguishing task-assignment criteria from the general criteria by which a person might be selected or appointed in the first place. A person’s general merit, reputation, or excellence in other domains does not by itself establish fitness for a particular task; that fitness must be determined with reference to the work itself.

Hitopadesha 3.56 offers a concise corroboration: यो यत्र कुशलः कार्ये तं तत्र विनियोजयेत् \| कर्मस्वदृष्टकर्मा यः शास्त्रज्ञोऽपि विमुह्यति \|\|. The first line closely echoes the principle of placing a person in the work for which he is competent. The second adds a caution: even someone learned in the Shastras may falter when unfamiliar with the practical work. This reinforces the distinction between knowing a subject in theory and being competent to perform the particular task.

Yajnavalkya Smriti 1.322 similarly directs the appointment of officials with domain-specific expertise: तत्र तत्र च निष्णातानध्यक्षान् कुशलान् शुचीन् \| प्रकुर्यादायकर्मान्तव्ययकर्मसु चोद्यतान् \|\|. The expression तत्र तत्र च निष्णातान्—experts in their respective fields—supports the kural’s insistence on matching the person to the work.

Kautilya’s Arthashastra 1.8.28 adds a related but distinct principle: कार्यसामर्थ्याद्धि पुरुषसामर्थ्यं कल्प्यते—a person’s competence is inferred from their demonstrated ability in the work. Read alongside Kautilya, the உரிமை in Valluvar’s வினைக்குரிமை நாடுதல் may be understood to go beyond mere कौशलम् (skill or competence as an attribute): fitness for a task rests not merely on possessing a relevant skill, but on evidence of that skill in practice.


### Verse 9

I propose Pañcatantra 1.382 as the primary parallel for this kural. Valluvar’s வினைக்கண் வினையுடையான், the person devoted to his work, corresponds to भक्तं शक्तम्, the devoted and capable servant, while கேண்மைவேறாக நினைப்பானை, misjudging the relationship with such a worker, finds its counterpart in न भृत्यमवमानयेत्, the injunction not to slight a servant: भक्तं शक्तं कुलीनं च न भृत्यमवमानयेत् । पुत्रव लालयेन्नित्यं य इच्छेच्छ्रियमात्मनः \|\|. The verse says that one should not insult a devoted, capable, high-born servant; one should cherish him like a son, if one desires one’s own prosperity. 

The phrase கேண்மைவேறாக recalls mitrabheda itself: கேண்மை corresponds to mitra (friendship), and வேறாக to bheda (separation). This near-literal correspondence is a striking breadcrumb, especially since the Panchatantra verse appears within the Mitrabheda tantra. Though not the primary correspondence, it reinforces the connection between Valluvar’s warning against alienating a dutiful worker and the Panchatantra’s concern with the rupture of relationships.  

The kural foregrounds the worker’s diligence and warns against misconstruing the relationship arising from his devoted service; the Panchatantra foregrounds the master’s obligation to cherish such a servant पुत्रवत्, like a son. Both connect the treatment of the worker with prosperity: in the kural, திரு departs when the relationship is misjudged; in the Panchatantra, the desire to preserve श्री motivates the injunction against mistreatment. The Sanskrit states the positive rule, while Valluvar states the adverse consequence of violating it. This reading accords with Parimelazhakar’s interpretation.

Parimelazhakar’s commentary makes the relational dimension explicit and adds the possibility of intrigue by jealous third parties: எப்பொழுதும் தன் வினையின்கண்ணே முயறலை உடையான் அவ்வுரிமையால் தனக்குக் கேளாய் ஒழுகுகின்ற தன்மையை; அது பொறாதார் சொற்கேட்டு அரசன் மாறுபடக் கருதுமாயின், திருமகள் அவனை விட்டு நீங்கும். கேளாய் ஒழுகுகின்ற தன்மையாவது: தான் பிறனாய் நில்லாது கேளிர்செய்தொழுகும் அவனை அவமதிப்பாகக் கொண்டு செறக்கருதுமாயின், பின் ஒருவரும்உட்பட்டு முயல்வார் இல்லையாம். ஆகவே, தன் செல்வம்கெடும் என்பது கருத்து. இந்நான்கு பாட்டானும் ஆடற்குரியானைஆளும் திறம் கூறப்பட்டது. The commentary explains that the worker, through his commitment to his duties, behaves as one belonging to the ruler’s own circle rather than as an outsider. If the ruler, listening to those who resent this closeness, comes to regard the worker differently and seeks to punish him, prosperity will depart: others will no longer be willing to participate wholeheartedly in the work. The jealous informants and the resulting change of mind on the ruler’s part are additional elements in Parimelazhakar’s explanation; they are not explicit in the couplet itself.

A complementary warning appears in the Mahabharata, Shanti Parva 12.94.16–17: मूढमैन्द्रियकं लुब्धमनार्यचरितं शठम् \| अनतीतोपधं हिंस्रं दुर्बुद्धिमबहुश्रुतम् \|\| त्यक्तोपात्तं मद्यरतं द्यूतस्त्रीमृगयापरम् \| कार्ये महति यो युञ्ज्याद्धीयते स नृपः श्रियः \|\|. A king who assigns an important undertaking to a foolish, greedy, deceitful, harmful, poorly judged, and otherwise disqualified person loses prosperity. The passage complements the present kural by showing how the ruler’s handling of personnel affects the state’s prosperity, but its focus is different: the Mahābhārata warns against entrusting a major task to an unsuitable person, whereas Valluvar warns against misconstruing the relationship with a capable and dutiful worker.

Arthashastra 9.4.1–3 provides a broader framework for evaluating the costs and benefits of state action: युग्यपुरुषापचयः क्षयः \|\| (9.4.1), the loss of capable personnel, is a form of depletion; हिरण्यधान्यापचयो व्ययः ॥ (9.4.2), the depletion of gold and grain, is an expenditure; and ताभ्यां बहुगुणविशिष्टे लाभे यायात् \|\| (9.4.3), one should proceed when the anticipated gain substantially outweighs these two costs. Valluvar’s calculus addresses a specific case within this broader problem space: treating a dutiful employee as an outsider leads to the departure of prosperity (திரு). Where Kautilya frames the ruler’s decision in terms of weighing gains against the potential loss of personnel and material resources, Valluvar draws attention to how a particular personnel-relations choice itself carries an economic consequence.


### Verse 10

I propose Kautilya’s Arthashastra 2.9.2 as the primary parallel for this Kural. The first half, நாடோறு நாடுக மன்னன் வினைசெய்வான், corresponds closely to Kautilya’s injunction, कर्मसु चैषां नित्यं परीक्षां कारयेत्, prescribing continual examination of officials in the performance of their duties. Kautilya explains the reason for this oversight: चित्तानित्यत्वात् मनुष्याणाम्—the minds of human beings are inconstant. This inconstancy suggests the possibility of their going astray, making continual supervision the means by which their கோடாமை is ensured. The general observation about human inconstancy also resonates with கோடாமை கோடா உலகு, though the kural’s specific formulation—that the realm remains unerring through the functionary’s non-deviation—is not an exact correspondence to Kautilya’s statement.

The Mahabharata, Shanti Parva (BORI) 12.120.30, corroborates the first half and offers broader contextual support: नित्यं राष्ट्रमवेक्षेत गोभिः सूर्य इवोत्पतन् \| चारांश्च नचरान्विद्यात्तथा बुद्ध्या न संज्वरेत् \|\| — the king should constantly survey the realm, like the sun moving with its rays, and discern the activities of agents and non-agents. The नित्यम् ... अवेक्षेत directly corroborates நாடோறு நாடுக, while राष्ट्र provides a contextual parallel for the realm denoted by உலகு, which traditional commentaries on the kural gloss as நாடு. The image of the sun’s pervasive, recurring passage across the world resonates with நாடோறு நாடுக and உலகு. Read against the Vedic association of the Sun with Rta (ऋत), the cosmic order, it also offers a deeper conceptual resonance with கோடாமை கோடா உலகு: just as the Sun’s regular course sustains cosmic order, the functionary’s fidelity and the king’s vigilant supervision sustain order in the realm. The sun’s illumination also evokes the modern metaphor of transparency: by bringing conduct to light, public scrutiny can help expose and deter misconduct, offering a further interpretive resonance with கோடாமை.

A related emphasis on personal supervision appears in Mahabharata, Shanti Parva (BORI) 12.120.28: अमोघक्रोधहर्षस्य स्वयं कृत्यान्ववेक्षिणः । आत्मप्रत्ययकोशस्य वसुधैव वसुंधरा ॥ — the ruler who personally reviews affairs and maintains a treasury grounded in self-reliance finds the earth itself a sustaining foundation. This reinforces the value of direct oversight, though its concluding image concerns the earth as the king’s support, not the realm’s stability through a functionary’s fidelity.


### Chapter Summary

This chapter concerns the discernment required to select people for responsibility, match them to appropriate tasks, delegate work judiciously, and maintain effective oversight. The Arthashastra provides the principal framework through which much of the chapter can be read, with its extensive treatment of administration, personnel, and the management of state resources. Other niti and dharma texts largely converge on these governing principles, while their distinctive formulations, examples, and metaphors illuminate additional dimensions of Valluvar’s compact and sometimes cryptic couplets.

The table above summarizes the proposed primary parallels as well as correspondences with the Arthashastra. Valluvar’s chapter brings particular precepts of statecraft into sharp focus through ten compact couplets. Kautilya’s Arthashastra, with its much wider and deeper treatment of governance, administration, and political economy, supplies a larger framework within which several of those precepts can be situated. The relationship is one of a broad congruence of principles, expressed at different scales and for different purposes. Our aim is to identify genuine correspondences while preserving differences in emphasis, formulation, and purpose.

The corroborating texts offer several further insights into the principles compressed into these couplets:

  - **Character and integrity:** The Arthashastra’s *upadha* tests show how the assessment of a prospective minister could extend beyond outward merit to integrity under different kinds of pressure. They illuminate the wider administrative context of Valluvar’s concern with character without requiring a direct equivalence between the tests and the qualities enumerated in the Kural.
  - **Means and execution:** Kautilya’s four *upayas*—conciliation, inducement, division, and coercion—give a specific political dimension to the means available for accomplishing a task. Read alongside his consideration of personnel and material resources, they help bring out the breadth of Valluvar’s compact expression இதனால்.
  - **Competence and task-fit:** The Hitopadesha, Matsya Purana, and Arthashastra reinforce the distinction between general merit and demonstrated suitability for particular work. Kautilya’s comparison with horses adds a memorable reminder that differences in capacity may become evident only in performance.
  - **Trust and prosperity:** The Panchatantra’s warning against insulting a devoted, capable servant illuminates kural 9’s concern with the relationship between a ruler and a diligent worker. The phrase கேண்மைவேறாக echoes Mitrabheda—கேண்மை (mitra, friendship) and வேறாக (bheda, separation)—a striking lexical breadcrumb linking Valluvar’s warning to the Panchatantra’s first tantra. The Arthashastra’s broader accounting of personnel loss, material expenditure, and anticipated gain then places Valluvar’s specific warning within the larger economic consequences of state action.
  - **Selection and continuing scrutiny:** The Mahabharata’s imagery of the king surveying the realm, together with Kautilya’s insistence on regularly examining officials, reinforces the chapter’s closing emphasis on vigilance. Selection is not a once-and-for-all judgment; responsibility requires continuing observation.

These parallels do not make the texts interchangeable, nor do they imply that every nuance is explicit in Valluvar. Rather, they show how the wider niti tradition can illuminate the concentrated force of his formulations while preserving the distinct contribution of each text.

### Discernment and நன்மை பயக்கும்: a tripartite reading of the Tirukkural

The title தெரிந்து வினையாடல் suggests a connection between discernment and action, a theme that can be illuminated by the Kathopanishad’s distinction between shreyas and preyas (1.2.2). This offers a broad conceptual lens across the Arattuppal, Porutpal, and Kamattuppal, provided that shreyas and preyas are not simplistically equated with a universal moral opposition between good and evil. The central concern is discerning what is genuinely beneficial rather than merely attractive or immediately desirable.

  - **Arattuppal:** இருமை வகைதெரிந்து (Kural 23) expresses discernment between alternatives in the pursuit of virtue. Yet Kural 292—பொய்மையும் வாய்மை யிடத்த புரைதீர்ந்த நன்மை பயக்கும் எனின்—shows that an apparently absolute distinction between truth and falsehood may be qualified when falsehood yields faultless good. The operative criterion is நன்மை பயக்கும்: that which brings about a beneficial outcome.
  - **Porutpal:** நன்மையுந் தீமையு நாடி நலம்புரிந்த தன்மையான், in the present chapter, applies discernment to governance. The ruler must examine alternatives and favor what serves genuine welfare. Arthashastra 1.19.34 articulates the governing criterion: प्रजासुखे सुखं राज्ञः प्रजानां च हिते हितम्—“In the happiness of the subjects lies the king’s happiness; in their welfare lies his welfare.” The ruler’s judgment is therefore directed toward the good of those he governs, not merely toward personal preference. This allows for the use of means that might otherwise appear questionable, such as deception and espionage, when they serve the welfare of the realm.
  - **Kamattuppal:** The same interpretive lens may be extended, more speculatively, to ஊடல் and கூடல். Their apparent opposition is not absolute: sulking or feigned anger may deepen affection, while reconciliation brings a different pleasure. Their value depends on how they function within the relationship.

Across the three sections, இருமை வகைதெரிந்து in Arattuppal may be read as discerning between alternatives in the pursuit of virtue; நன்மையுந் தீமையு நாடி in Porutpal as weighing alternatives in governance; and the treatment of ஊடல் and கூடல் in Kamattuppal as recognizing the different pleasures and functions of apparent opposites in love. The recurring principle may thus be stated as: discern the alternatives, consider their consequences in context, and favour what truly brings about நன்மை, understood here as the greater good or shreyas.

This is a synthetic interpretation of Valluvar’s thought, not a claim that every occurrence of நன்மை, தீமை, அறம், or shreyas and preyas in the proposed parallels has precisely the same meaning in every context.

### Concluding thoughts

Taken together, the correspondences reveal a broad congruence between Valluvar and the Sanskrit niti tradition: sound governance depends on discerning the good, selecting and matching capable people to particular work, delegating with judgment, and maintaining oversight. The Arthashastra supplies the larger framework, while Valluvar’s ten compact couplets selectively distill particular principles within it, preserving their own emphasis and force.
